# Noise-aware guidance models and better gradient paths for CLIP guidance of OpenAI's 256x256 unconditional ImageNet U-Net

Scope: (a) training or reusing a noise-aware or time-conditioned guide, so that guidance carries information at high noise, and (b) other ways to compute the guidance gradient: which clean-image estimate it uses, and what it backpropagates through. Target: `256x256_diffusion_uncond.pt`, guided by `cgd/adm.py` and `cgd/sampler.py`. Unconditional runs use `UNCONDITIONAL_GUIDANCE_WINDOW = (0.1, 1.0)`, so guidance starts at the first step, and use 60 calls. Hardware: 8 GB RTX 2070 SUPER for inference, 12 GB RTX 4070 for training.

These notes build on `research_notes/CLIP guided diffusion quality improvements/`, which already covered several items: the secondary-model measurement, GLIDE checkpoint facts, MPGD/TFG/EluCD numbers, normalisation and the FARE gradient statistics. Facts taken from there carry the original paper's URL. Estimates are labelled **[estimate]**. Measurements from this repo are labelled **[measured here]** and cite the local file.

## Q1. Noised CLIP: how GLIDE trained it, how it compared with CFG, what checkpoints exist, and whether a 256px noise-aware CLIP can be trained or distilled on 12 GB

### Takeaway
GLIDE's noised CLIP took internet-scale compute: 390K iterations at batch 32K, at 64x64 only. In GLIDE's own human evaluation it lost to classifier-free guidance, and its only comparison against the 2021 CLIP-guided notebooks is qualitative and confounded. Only that 64px ViT-B checkpoint is public. The strongest evidence that a cheap noise-aware guide works on this exact model is PPAP, which ran on OpenAI's unconditional ADM 256. It distilled a clean classifier into noise-band experts with no labels, on generated images, and FID went from 40.24 to 27.86 at 25 DDIM steps, where plain x̂0 gradients reached only 38.14. A distilled noise-aware CLIP student on a 12 GB GPU is plausible in 1 to 3 days **[estimate]**. Nobody has published one for pixel-space CLIP guidance, though. The one latent-space comparison found a time-conditioned CLIP scoring *below* plain CLIP on one-step-denoised images.

### Cited Findings
**GLIDE's noised CLIP**
- Image encoder `f(x_t, t)` at 64x64, a ViT with 4x4 patches. The main model is a ViT-L with weight decay 0.0125. It keeps CLIP's contrastive objective and uses the base model's noise schedule — [GLIDE, Sec. 2.4, 4, App. B.1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.10741)
- Training: 390K iterations at batch size 32K on a 50/50 mix of the CLIP and DALL-E datasets, then 30K iterations of fine-tuning on "an even broader dataset of internet images." The paper does not state the learning rate — [GLIDE App. B.1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.10741)
- Guidance: `μ̂ = μ + s·Σ·∇_{x_t}(f(x_t)·g(c))`, with s = 2.0 in the main comparison — [GLIDE Sec. 2.4](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.10741)
- Human Elo on MS-COCO 256x256, 1,000 comparisons per attribute (photorealism / caption): unguided −88.6 / −106.2, CLIP guidance s=2.0 −73.2 / 29.3, classifier-free guidance s=3.0 82.7 / 110.9. CLIP guidance raises the CLIP score far more than CFG does, but humans disagree with the CLIP score. GLIDE hypothesises that CLIP guidance finds adversarial examples for the evaluating CLIP — [GLIDE Table 1, Sec. 5.2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.10741)
- Appendix D: a noised ViT-B guides a 64x64 ImageNet model at a fixed scale of 15.0, followed by an upsampler. GLIDE says it gives "equal or higher quality" than Crowson's unnoised-CLIP notebook, which uses augmentations and auxiliary losses. The comparison is **qualitative only and confounded**, because only GLIDE's pipeline had the upsampler — [GLIDE App. D, Fig. 10](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.10741)
- Public weights: `clip_image_enc.pt` (345.8 MB) and `clip_text_enc.pt` (286.8 MB). Config: 64px, 512-d embedding, width 768, 12 blocks, cosine schedule, 1000 steps, logit scale 100. Trained on 137M filtered pairs with no people. MIT licensed. The repo was archived on 2026-05-29 — [openai/glide-text2im](https://github.com/openai/glide-text2im), as recorded in the earlier notes
- A 64px noise-aware CLIP fits this model's composition stage. Area-pooling a 256px `x_t` by 4x4 multiplies the SNR of white noise by 16, so `ᾱ' = 16·SNR/(1+16·SNR)` maps to GLIDE's cosine timestep — derivation in the earlier notes, [losses_and_ensembles.md §6](../CLIP%20guided%20diffusion%20quality%20improvements/losses_and_ensembles.md)

**Other noise-aware CLIP-like encoders**
- **SDG** fine-tunes a CLIP RN50x16 image encoder with a *self-supervised* contrastive loss. Positives are clean and noised versions of the same image, and the clean encoder is frozen as the teacher. t is injected by converting BatchNorm into adaptive BN whose scale and bias are predicted from t. Batch 256, LR 1e-4, weight decay 1e-3. It was trained per dataset on FFHQ and LSUN, never ImageNet. The paper reports **no** comparison against the original CLIP as a guide — [SDG](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.05744)
- **NoisyCLIP (Dec 2025):** fine-tunes only the ViT-L/14 visual encoder, with InfoNCE against frozen text, on about 50K SDXL noisy latents from steps 20 to 29 of 50. 10 epochs, batch 128, LR 5.7e-5, **one A100 40GB**. On noisy latents R@1 is 0.506 against 0.145 for plain CLIP L/14. Best-of-N VQAScore from mid-generation is 0.709, against 0.657 for plain CLIP and 0.747 for final-image CLIP. It is used only for ranking, not as a guidance gradient, and no checkpoints are released — [NoisyCLIP](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08505v1)
- **Time-conditioned latent CLIP (Sep 2025):** a ViT-B/16 (194M with text tower), conditioned on t and trained contrastively on noisy latents for 90K steps at batch 512, on 64 TPUv5e for 1.5 days. Used as a filter, it scored **below** a pixel CLIP applied to one-step-denoised decoded images at every point (Gecko score). LDM-small at 25% of sampling: 39.7 latent against 43.4 pixel. LDM-large at 50%: 45.2 against 48.9 — [Dynamic CFG via online feedback, Table 1, App. A.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16131)
- **StitchVM (May 2026):** builds a noisy-input value function `V_t ≈ E[reward(x0) | x_t]`. It stitches the frozen early layers of a diffusion backbone (already noise-aware) to the later layers of a pixel reward model (CLIP, HPSv2, Aesthetic Predictor). Only a linear-initialised stitching layer and the reward tail are trained, in about 10 GPU-hours at 512 on GH200. The authors claim 3.2x faster DPS-style guidance than Tweedie+decode+reward backprop, with about 50% lower peak memory and higher quality on "nearly every" reward×metric pair. They also claim it "substantially" beats NoisyCLIP, shown only in figures. These are author claims, not independently verified — [StitchVM project page](https://gohyojun15.github.io/StitchVM); [listing, arXiv 2605.19804](https://www.opentrain.ai/papers/stitched-value-model-for-diffusion-alignment--arxiv-2605.19804/)

**Distilling a clean guide into a noise-aware one on this exact diffusion model (PPAP, CVPR 2023)**
- Main experiments use the **unconditional ADM trained on ImageNet 256x256** from Dhariwal & Nichol. Guides are ResNet50 and DeiT-S — [PPAP](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.05973)
- Recipe: 5 experts by default, each covering a band of 200 timesteps out of 1000 (10 experts was best in the ablation). Each expert is a LoRA of the off-the-shelf model: ResNet50 rank 16 plus bias and BN tuning, DeiT-S rank 32 on attention. Data-free knowledge transfer on about 500K images *generated by the diffusion model*. Loss is the KL between the teacher's temperature-scaled softmax on the clean image and the expert's softmax on its noised version. AdamW at LR 1e-4, weight decay 0.05, batch 256, 300K iterations, on 8xA100 — [PPAP §4–5](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.05973)
- Results with ResNet50 guiding, DDIM 25 steps, as FID / IS: unguided 40.24/34.53, naive off-the-shelf 38.74/33.95, **gradients on x̂0 38.14/33.77**, single noise-aware model fully fine-tuned on labelled ImageNet 30.42/43.05, multi-experts-5 on labelled ImageNet 19.98/74.78, PPAP-5 data-free 29.65/44.23, PPAP-10 data-free 27.86/46.74 — [PPAP Table 1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.05973)
- With DDPM 250 steps: unguided 28.97, naive 29.03, x̂0 gradients 28.81, **single noise-aware 38.15 (worse than unguided)**, multi-experts-5 16.37, PPAP-10 21.00. DeiT-S shows the same pattern: single noise-aware 36.01 against PPAP-10 20.07 — [PPAP Table 1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.05973)
- Ablations: t-conditioning barely matters. Multi-experts-5 gives 19.98 with it and 19.54 without, and single noise-aware 30.08 with and 30.42 without. Capacity per noise band matters: 5 ResNet18 experts reach 20.38, against 29.63 for one ResNet152. Data-free against labelled for multi-experts: 29.87 against 19.98 — [PPAP Tables 2–5](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.05973)

**Dhariwal & Nichol's noisy classifiers, as the training-cost reference**
- Architecture: the downsampling trunk of the U-Net with attention pooling at 8x8, trained on the diffusion model's own noising distribution with t as input, and random crops against overfitting — [ADM](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.05233)
- Compute (Table 10): 256px classifier 46 V100-days, against 916 for its generator. 128px classifier 9 V100-days. A 150K-iteration 256 classifier took 14 V100-days — [ADM Table 10](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.05233)
- Hyperparameters (Table 12, partially read): batch 256, LR 3e-4, weight decay 0.05, 300K to 500K iterations. The first column had batch 1024, LR 6e-4, weight decay 0.2 and 300K. The excerpt did not show the column-to-resolution mapping — [ADM Table 12](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.05233)
- Classifier guidance of the **unconditional** 256 model, both trained 2M iterations (FID / sFID / IS / P / R): unguided 26.21/6.35/39.70/0.61/0.63, scale 1.0 33.03/6.99/32.92/0.56/0.65, scale 10.0 12.00/10.40/95.41/0.76/0.44. The unconditional model needs a large scale for a noise-aware guide to help — [ADM Table 4](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.05233)

### Inferences
- GLIDE trained its noised CLIP from scratch on about 12.5 to 12.8 billion samples (390K × 32K). That is not reproducible here, and it should not be the model to copy. The feasible pattern is PPAP's: keep the clean model as the teacher, train small adapters per noise band, and distil clean-input outputs into noised-input outputs.
- **A noise-aware CLIP student recipe for 12 GB [estimate]:**
  - Teacher: the same CLIP tower that guides, frozen on clean images. Student: the same tower with LoRA (rank 16 to 32) plus a t-embedding, or 4 to 5 band experts covering the guided window.
  - Data: real images (ImageNet train, 1.28M, or CC3M), noised with ADM's linear schedule at 256 and then resized to 224. Real data needs no generation pass. PPAP's labelled-against-data-free gap (19.98 against 29.87) suggests real data is better than generated data.
  - Loss: cosine or MSE to the teacher's clean embedding, optionally with InfoNCE to captions as in NoisyCLIP. Under MSE the student learns `E[φ(x0) | x_t]`, so `∇_{x_t}⟨student(x_t), e_text⟩` is the gradient of the *expected* CLIP similarity under the posterior. That is a first-order value function, which is what guidance at high noise should follow.
  - The expected embedding shrinks in norm at high noise, since it averages over many images. The spherical loss's normalisation would rescale it, so use the unnormalised dot product, or rely on the repo's existing cap.
  - Cost: ViT-B/16 forward at 224 is about 17.6 GFLOPs (the commonly quoted DeiT-B figure, not checked here). A training step with a teacher forward is about 70 GFLOPs per image. An RTX 4070 at bf16 with FP32 accumulate peaks around 58 TFLOPS. At 35 to 40% utilisation that is about 300 images/s, or 25M images per day. PPAP's 300K × 256 = 77M samples would take about 3 days. ViT-B/32 is about 4x cheaper, so under a day. Memory: ViT-B with LoRA and gradient checkpointing at batch 128 fits 12 GB comfortably.
  - Evaluation: compare against the repo's current x̂0 path on held-out prompts with the existing judges. NoisyCLIP's R@1 jump (0.145 to 0.506) shows the student can be measured cheaply before any sampling run.
- **Inference cost on 8 GB:** a guide that reads `x_t` directly lets the U-Net run under `no_grad`, removing the single most expensive part of guidance. The earlier measurement put unguided 256 at 1.91 GiB peak against 5.33 with U-Net backprop. A noise-aware ViT-B/16 student guide should therefore peak around 2.5 to 3 GiB, and approach unguided speed **[estimate, from [refinement_pipelines.md §2](../CLIP%20guided%20diffusion%20quality%20improvements/refinement_pipelines.md)]**.
- **Where it should help most:** the unconditional model sets composition at high noise, where x̂0 is a blurry posterior mean. A noise-aware student is trained exactly there, so use it for the first part of the window (σ above about 0.9) and keep CLIP-through-x̂0 for the rest. PPAP shows "gradients on x̂0" from an off-the-shelf model barely move FID (38.74 to 38.14), while noise-band experts move it a lot. That is the strongest evidence on this model that a noise-aware guide beats x̂0 gradients from a clean guide. It concerns ImageNet classes, not text, though.
- **Caution:** GLIDE (Elo) and the latent-CLIP filter (Gecko) are the two cases where a noise-trained CLIP was compared fairly, and neither shows it beating "plain CLIP on a denoised image" on quality. GLIDE lost to CFG, which this repo cannot use. The latent CLIP lost to pixel CLIP on one-step x0. Expect a student to help mainly at the highest noise levels, and test it as an additive term, not a replacement.
- **StitchVM-style alternative specific to ADM [inference]:** the ADM U-Net's encoder half already runs every step, and it is noise-aware. Dhariwal & Nichol's classifier *is* that downsampling trunk. A head made of the U-Net's input and middle blocks, plus a small trained stitch into CLIP's later ViT blocks, would give a noisy-input CLIP whose gradient backpropagates through only half the U-Net. Training touches only the stitch and the tail. It is untested anywhere for ADM.
- **Zero-training option:** GLIDE's public 64px noised CLIP, with 4x4 pooling of `x_t`, as an extra term at high noise only. It is ViT-B and trained on filtered data with no people, so it should judge composition, not detail.

### Gaps
- No public noise-aware CLIP at 224px or higher was found, and no paper reports FID or CLIP score for a noise-aware CLIP against plain CLIP-through-x̂0 *on the same pixel diffusion model*.
- GLIDE's learning rate and the noised ViT-B's training budget are not given in the paper.
- PPAP reports no wall-clock time. Its GLIDE results are qualitative.
- SDG's iteration count and compute are not reported.
- StitchVM's numeric tables were not read, only the project page. Its NoisyCLIP comparison is figures only.
- The time-conditioned latent CLIP paper reports no accuracy against noise level for its own encoder.
- A lead not followed: PixelDiT2 (arXiv 2609.24919) keeps a pretrained encoder frozen and adds a timestep-conditioned projector for noisy inputs. Only a search summary was seen.

## Q2. Disco Diffusion's secondary model: training, effect on speed and quality, and practitioner verdict

### Takeaway
How `secondary_model_imagenet_2.pth` was trained is undocumented in every primary source found. It is a 13.8M-parameter, attention-free, v-prediction U-Net on a cosine schedule. It was added to Disco on 2021-11-22 and defaulted on, including for the 256 unconditional model, and Replicate's hosted Disco also defaults it on. That default is the only "practitioner verdict" on record. No Reddit thread, cheatsheet or paper with a measured quality comparison was found. Locally it tied at 256, trended slightly negative at 512, and cut peak VRAM from 5.3 to 1.9 GiB. Nothing found suggests the result would differ on the unconditional model, though that was not measured.

### Cited Findings
- Architecture from code: `c=64`, channels 64→512, no attention, Fourier time features, predicts v, `pred = x·α − v·σ`, time `t = atan2(σ, α)·2/π`, so it uses a cosine-schedule time parameterisation. Disco maps ADM's `x_t` to it through the schedule's α and σ — [disco.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alembics/disco-diffusion/main/disco.py), as recorded in the earlier notes
- MMagic's port describes it only as a "smaller secondary diffusion model trained by Katherine Crowson to remove noise from intermediate timesteps to prepare them for CLIP" — [MMagic source](https://mmagic.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/mmagic/models/editors/disco_diffusion/secondary_model.html)
- History: "v2 Update: Nov 22nd 2021 – Somnai… Initial addition of Katherine Crowson's Secondary Model Method". The provenance section says Disco uses "either OpenAI's 256x256 unconditional ImageNet or Katherine Crowson's fine-tuned 512x512 diffusion model" — [disco-diffusion README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alembics/disco-diffusion/main/README.md); [htoyryla gist](https://gist.github.com/htoyryla/23bbce2053cb673d3ba53832960dc5bd)
- DALL-E 2 cites the method as "CLIP Guided Diffusion 512x512, Secondary Model Method", shared on Twitter and never published as a paper — [DALL-E 2 refs](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.06125)
- Defaults: `use_secondary_model = True` in Disco. Loading does not depend on which diffusion model is chosen, so the 256 unconditional model also got it by default. Replicate's `nightmareai/disco-diffusion` lists "Use secondary model", default `True` — [disco.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alembics/disco-diffusion/main/disco.py); [Replicate API](https://replicate.com/nightmareai/disco-diffusion/versions/f33f6828007304372ba05eb677d766d6f970c82dea84de9f1c7c720d424605cd/api)
- Weights: 13,841,547 parameters. The original hosts are dead. A Hugging Face mirror matches Disco's pinned sha256 `983e3de6…613a`, at 55,380,626 bytes — **[measured here]**, [refinement_pipelines.md §2](../CLIP%20guided%20diffusion%20quality%20improvements/refinement_pipelines.md)
- **[measured here]**, guidance through the secondary model with the U-Net still giving the sampler's x0, deltas with 95% CI. At 256: time 8.02 to 5.80 s, peak 5.33 to 1.92 GiB, ViT-B/16 −.011 [−.026, +.004], ViT-H +.008 [−.013, +.030], SigLIP2 +.005 [−.008, +.019]. At 512: time 18.73 to 10.80 s, peak 4.13 to 2.64 GiB, ViT-B/16 −.012 [−.025, −.001]. The README summary: −0.005 ± 0.023 at 256 on 2 seeds, −0.009 ± 0.022 at 512, −0.019 ± 0.022 at 128 — [README "Tried and removed"](/home/sam/GitHub/clip-guided-diffusion/README.md); [refinement_pipelines.md §2](../CLIP%20guided%20diffusion%20quality%20improvements/refinement_pipelines.md)

### Inferences
- The secondary model is an independent ImageNet denoiser, judging by its name and Disco's use. It does not imitate ADM. Its x0 is its *own* posterior mean, from a lower-capacity network, so at high noise it is at least as blurry as ADM's. Its Jacobian is that of a small convolutional net. On the unconditional model, where the problem is a weak gradient at high noise, it trades a weaker x0 for speed and memory, and does not attack the problem. That fits the tie or slight loss measured on the class-conditional model.
- The freed VRAM (about 3.4 GiB at 256) is the useful part. It could pay for an ensemble or heavier guide, or for a multi-step rollout (Q4), within 8 GB.
- **An upgrade with a reason to beat the original [inference]:** train a "secondary model v2" on the ADM uncond model's own *ODE endpoints* instead of noisy-real pairs. Every state on one deterministic trajectory shares the same endpoint, so one 25-call DPM-Solver++ trajectory yields 25 (x_t, x0) training pairs. 100K trajectories give 2.5M pairs. At roughly 1 to 2 s per trajectory batched on the 4070, that is 1 to 2 days of generation, plus about a day of training a 14M to 60M U-Net **[estimate]**. Its x0 would be a sharp, in-support sample, not a blurry mean. Xu et al. found that this helps neural-network guides (Q4).

### Gaps
- No primary source states the secondary model's training data, objective details, iterations or compute. The name only suggests ImageNet.
- The "50% faster" figure often repeated about the secondary model could not be traced to a primary source. Zippy's cheatsheet could not be retrieved.
- No measured comparison of the secondary model against U-Net backprop on an *unconditional* model was found, in the community or in papers.

## Q3. Time-dependent classifiers, Elucidating classifier guidance, off-the-shelf calibration, and 2024 to 2026 work on noise-robust guides

### Takeaway
On class-conditional ADM 128, off-the-shelf classifiers with calibration *beat* OpenAI's noise-trained classifier: EluCD got FID 2.19 with ResNet-101 against 2.97 fine-tuned, and the Smooth-ECE work of Nov 2025 reached 2.13. On the *unconditional* 256 model, though, PPAP found off-the-shelf and x̂0 gradients nearly useless, and noise-band experts strong. That supports a noise-aware or band-specialised guide where the generator has no class prior, which is the repo's unconditional case. Since 2024 the main thrust has been making training-free guidance smoother (random augmentation, Polyak steps, spherical constraints, calibration) rather than training noise-robust guides. One 2026 exception, StitchVM, builds noisy-input reward models cheaply.

### Cited Findings
- **EluCD (ICLR 2024)**, off-the-shelf classifier guidance on ADM ImageNet 128, 250 DDPM steps. Softplus β=3 improves FID 7.17 to 6.61. Joint/marginal temperature τ2 1.0 to 0.5 improves 6.20 to 5.27. A sine schedule `γ_t = σ_t + γσ_T sin(πt/T)` with γ 0 to 0.3 improves 5.57 to 5.24. 50K FID: baseline 5.91, fine-tuned noisy classifier 2.97, CFG 2.43, ResNet-50 2.36, ResNet-101 2.19. It reports CLIP guidance of conditional ADM with ViT-L/336 running about 5x faster than Universal Guidance, with "significantly higher" CLIP scores from the sine schedule, shown only as a plot — [EluCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11311), as recorded in the earlier notes
- **Calibration and regularisation (Nov 2025):** observes that guided classifiers are overconfident in early steps, so the gradient vanishes. Fixes: a differentiable Smooth ECE fine-tune, tilted sampling, entropy-regularised sampling, and f-divergence guidance. FID 2.13 on ImageNet 128 with ResNet-101, no diffusion retraining — [arXiv 2511.05844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05844)
- **Shen et al. (NeurIPS 2024):** theory says that for a loss bounded by C, the noise-smoothed loss is `C·√(2/(πσ_t²))`-Lipschitz, so time-dependent classifiers are less prone to adversarial gradients than off-the-shelf ones. Training-free guidance also converges more slowly. Fixes are random augmentation over |𝒯| = 10 transforms and a Polyak step `η·‖ε_θ‖/‖g‖²·g`. On unconditional ImageNet with CLIP-B/16 guiding and CLIP-L/14 judging, 100 DDIM steps: LGD-MC 24.3, UG 25.7, FreeDoM 25.9, MPGD-Z 25.1, theirs 27.7 — [Shen et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12404v2)
- **TFG (NeurIPS 2024):** unifies DPS, LGD, UG, FreeDoM and MPGD. ImageNet label guidance at T=100 (accuracy % / FID): DPS 38.8/193, LGD 11.5/210, FreeDoM 19.7/200, MPGD 6.80/239, UGD 25.5/205, TFG 40.9/176 — [TFG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15761), as recorded in the earlier notes
- **Kawar et al.:** a robust noise-aware classifier on 128px takes FID from 2.97 to 2.85 — [Kawar et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2208.08664), as recorded in the earlier notes
- **PPAP** on unconditional ADM 256: see Q1. Off-the-shelf and x̂0 gradients improve FID at most from 40.24 to 38.14, while data-free noise-band experts reach 27.86 — [PPAP](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.05973)
- **StitchVM (2026):** a noisy-input value model from a frozen diffusion head plus a reward-model tail, in about 10 GPU-hours. See Q1 — [StitchVM](https://gohyojun15.github.io/StitchVM)
- **[measured here]** `create_classifier` in the crowsonkb `guided-diffusion` fork needs two runtime patches before OpenAI's classifiers load: `use_neighborhood_attention` in `ResBlock`, and `spatial=None` in `QKVAttention` — [losses_and_ensembles.md §6](../CLIP%20guided%20diffusion%20quality%20improvements/losses_and_ensembles.md)

### Inferences
- Class-conditional and unconditional generators give opposite verdicts on off-the-shelf against noise-aware guides. With a class-conditional generator, the generator supplies the prior at high noise and an off-the-shelf guide only has to polish. With an unconditional one, the guide must supply information at high noise, where off-the-shelf models see noise. The repo's unconditional docstring says the same: "CLIP also has to set the composition."
- EluCD's sine schedule and calibration act on *classifier* logits. CLIP's spherical loss has no softmax temperature to calibrate, except the logit scale if a contrastive form were used, and the repo found a contrastive loss lost (−0.010 ± 0.015). EluCD's schedule result is about the shape of the step size over time. The repo's own sine schedule test lost (−0.006 ± 0.009) — [README](/home/sam/GitHub/clip-guided-diffusion/README.md).
- The useful lesson from EluCD and the 2025 calibration paper is "the early-step gradient vanishes when the guide is overconfident or blind to noise." For CLIP that argues for a noise-aware student at high noise (Q1), not for re-weighting.

### Gaps
- No 2024 to 2026 paper was found that trains a noise-robust *CLIP* for pixel-space ImageNet guidance and reports text-alignment metrics.
- Full tables for the Smooth ECE paper (baselines, costs) were not read, only the abstract.
- The column-to-resolution mapping of ADM's classifier hyperparameters (Table 12) was cut off.

## Q4. Clean-image estimates and gradient paths: Tweedie x̂0, multi-step rollouts, consistency or distilled denoisers, and skipping the Jacobian

### Takeaway
Two kinds of evidence favour a better x0 than one-step Tweedie for neural-network guides. Multi-step x0 with exact gradients (SAG) gains up to about n=4. An in-support x0 from a consistency model (Xu et al.) raised classifier accuracy from 0.79 to 0.90 and lowered FID, for about 30% more time and 1 GB more VRAM. Skipping the U-Net Jacobian (MPGD) is cheap but collapses on hard semantic targets: 6.8% against DPS's 38.8% on ImageNet labels. On *unconditional ImageNet with CLIP*, the only head-to-head is Shen et al.: one-step x̂0 with time travel and random augmentation scored 27.7, against 25.1 to 25.9 for the alternatives. No paper compares multi-step x0 for CLIP guidance on that model. For 8 GB at 256, the affordable upgrade is a short no-grad or checkpointed rollout at high noise only.

### Cited Findings
**Multi-step x0 with gradients**
- **Symplectic Adjoint Guidance (SAG):** estimates x̂0 by n Euler steps of the PF-ODE and backpropagates exactly through them with a symplectic adjoint. n=1 is FreeDoM. It uses time travel and guides only a middle window. Memory on a V100 with SD: SAG 15.66 GB, adjoint 15.64, **direct backprop at n=2 28.63 GB**, so SAG's memory is nearly constant in n. Gains stop being significant past n=4, and n=4 to 5 is used — [SAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.12030v1)
- SAG results: style guidance (style loss ↓ / CLIP ↑) FreeDoM 482.7/22.37, UG 805/23.02, SAG 386.6/23.51. Face ID (ID loss / FID) FreeDoM 0.602/65.24, SAG 0.574/64.25. Aesthetic, column labelled "loss": SD 9.71, FreeDoM 9.18, DOODL 9.78, SAG 8.17. Object guidance CLIP-I/CLIP-T: FreeDoM .681/.281, DOODL .743/.277, SAG .774/.270. Per-image timings are not tabulated — [SAG Tables 1–2](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.12030v1)
- **DOODL:** backprop through the whole EDICT-invertible chain to the initial latent. CLIP score +3.1% and +2.6% over one-step classifier guidance on DrawBench DALL-E and Reddit prompts, in SD latent space — [DOODL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13703), as recorded in the earlier notes
- **Universal Guidance:** forward guidance on x̂0 plus a backward step of m optimiser steps on Δx0, with k-fold time travel. Its CLIP setting is s(t) = 10√(1−α_t), k = 8, m = 0, and the paper gives no quantitative text results. In Shen's benchmark it scored 25.7 on unconditional ImageNet — [UG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07121); [Shen](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12404v2)

**In-support x0 from a distilled one-step model**
- **Xu et al. (2024):** replaces DPS's posterior mean with a consistency model's output `x0|t = g_θ(t, x_t) + N(0, τ²)`, τ = 0.2. The argument is that the posterior mean "may not lie in the support of the image distribution," which degrades neural-network operators. Setup: LSUN Bedroom/Cat at 256, EDM base with 1000 Euler steps — [Xu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12063)
- Results on LSUN Bedroom, DPS against CM: segmentation mIoU 0.27 to 0.34 and FID 22.84 to 18.06. Layout mIoU 0.54 to 0.78, FID 7.59 to 7.50. BLIP captioning CLIP 22.57 to 22.63, FID 9.49 to 8.16. ResNet classification accuracy 0.79 to 0.90, FID 15.73 to 13.45. For a linear ×4 downsampling operator CM was only comparable: FID 4.82 against 5.67 — [Xu et al. Tables 1, 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12063)
- Cost: DPS 150 s and 5.35 GB, CM version 218 s and 6.32 GB — [Xu et al. Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12063)
- No public consistency model exists for ImageNet 256 *pixel* space distilled from ADM. OpenAI's CMs cover ImageNet 64 and LSUN 256, per the paper's setup and the consistency-model literature surveyed — [Xu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12063); [iCT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.14189)

**Skipping the Jacobian, and where x̂0 enters**
- **MPGD:** `x̂0 ← x̂0 − c_t ∇_{x̂0} L(x̂0)`, with no U-Net backward. Fine on easy pixel-near tasks: CelebA FaceID .5163 against FreeDoM's .5690, at 5.82 s against 10.65. On ImageNet label guidance it scored 6.80% / FID 239, against DPS 38.8% / 193 — [MPGD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16424); [TFG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15761)
- **FlowChef warning:** moving x_t while keeping a stale ε made IDDPM FID 19.28, against 5.39 baseline and 4.24 for MPGD at scale 1 — [FlowChef](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00100)
- **PPAP** on unconditional ADM 256: an off-the-shelf guide on x̂0 improves FID from 38.74 to 38.14 at DDIM-25, and makes DeiT-S worse at DDPM-250 (29.41 to 30.26) — [PPAP Table 1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.05973)
- **StitchVM** argues that Tweedie+decode+reward guidance carries a Jensen-gap bias that is worst at high noise, and that a noisy-input value function avoids it, with 3.2x faster DPS — author claim — [StitchVM](https://gohyojun15.github.io/StitchVM)
- **[measured here]** CLIP seeing only x̂0 instead of the blend: +0.002 ± 0.009 (7). The blend without its gradient: −0.004 ± 0.011 (4). The blend is effectively pure x̂0 for most of the window (x̂0 coefficient 0.997 at t=700) — [README](/home/sam/GitHub/clip-guided-diffusion/README.md); [practitioner_tricks_and_novel_ideas.md §4](../CLIP%20guided%20diffusion%20quality%20improvements/practitioner_tricks_and_novel_ideas.md)
- **[measured here]** The U-Net Jacobian is a strong low-pass. CLIP's raw gradient has 0.60 to 0.69 of its power above 0.25 cycles/pixel, and after the Jacobian 0.03 at σ 0.997 and 0.35 at σ 0.33 — [cutouts_and_gradient_noise.md](../CLIP%20guided%20diffusion%20quality%20improvements/cutouts_and_gradient_noise.md)

### Inferences
- **Why the unconditional model is the case for multi-step x0:** with no class label, x̂0 at σ ≈ 1 is close to the dataset mean, a grey blur. CLIP's gradient on it says little about composition, and the Jacobian then filters it further toward the lowest frequencies. A 2 to 4 step rollout to x0 at high noise gives CLIP an image with structure, and both SAG (n>1 helps, flat past 4) and Xu et al. (in-support x0 helps neural guides) point the same way. Neither tested CLIP on ADM, so this is the most promising untested path, not a proven one.
- **Cost on 8 GB at 256 [estimate]:** the current step does one U-Net forward with graph plus a backward, about 3 forward-equivalents, at 5.3 GiB peak. An n-step rollout with full backprop needs n graphs. SAG's memory doubled at n=2, so n ≥ 2 does not fit without checkpointing. Per-call checkpointing stores only each call's input and recomputes, keeping the peak near one call's graph (5.3 GiB plus small change). Time rises to about 1 + 3n forward-equivalents, so n=3 is roughly 3x the guided-step cost. Applied only while σ > 0.9, about the first quarter of 60 calls, a full run would cost about 1.5 to 2x the current time.
- **Cheaper hybrid [inference, untested]:** run the n-step rollout under `no_grad` to get a sharp x0_roll, then take CLIP's gradient at x0_roll and pass it back through the *one-step* Jacobian only. In effect, `∇_{x_t} ≈ (∂x̂0/∂x_t)ᵀ ∇_{x0} L(x0_roll)`. This costs n extra forwards and no extra memory. It is a straight-through estimator, and the MPGD/TFG evidence says the Jacobian matters for hard semantic targets, so keeping one Jacobian is the safer half.
- **Distilled ODE-endpoint denoiser:** the Q2 "secondary v2" gives a sharp x0 in one small forward, with a cheap Jacobian. It combines Xu et al.'s benefit with the secondary model's 1.9 GiB footprint. Adding τ ≈ 0.2 noise to its output follows Xu et al. and doubles as an anti-adversarial augmentation.
- **Skipping the Jacobian is not recommended for composition at high noise:** the label-guidance collapse is the closest analogue to "set the composition from text."

### Gaps
- No paper compares one-step Tweedie, multi-step rollout and a CM x0 for *CLIP text guidance on unconditional ImageNet ADM*. All the multi-step evidence is on SD, LSUN or faces.
- SAG's time cost against n is only in a figure. Its CLIP numbers come from style guidance, not text.
- "Training-free guidance via multi-step x0 estimation" as a standalone paper was not found beyond SAG. "Adjoint guidance" variants (AdjointDEIS and others) were not reviewed.
- No public ADM-256-uncond consistency or distilled one-step model was found.

## Q5. Gradient conditioning tricks: normalisation, Disco's clamp_grad/clamp_max and rand_mag, gradient denoising, momentum or Adam, adaptive schedules, spectral guidance

### Takeaway
Only a few of these tricks have quantitative support, and none of it is on CLIP guidance of ADM. DSG's spherical normalisation gives large gains at 20 to 50 steps on inverse problems and FaceID. Shen's random augmentation plus Polyak step helps on unconditional ImageNet CLIP, though without per-fix ablations. APG's projection and momentum help CFG. Adam with x̂0 helped in one small study. Disco's `clamp_grad` has no evidence, and `rand_mag` is not a gradient trick at all: it scales noise added to *prompt embeddings*. This repo's own tests already found that blurring the gradient, sine or rising schedules, and gradient-noise removal tie or lose, because the existing relative cap dominates.

### Cited Findings
- **Disco:** `clamp_grad` is described as "Experimental: Using adaptive clip grad in the cond_fn" and implemented as RMS clamping, `grad·min(‖g‖_rms, clamp_max)/‖g‖_rms`, with `clamp_max` 0.05 by default. `rand_mag` "Controls the magnitude of the random noise" and is used only in the `fuzzy_prompt` branch, as `(txt + randn·rand_mag).clamp(0,1)` on text embeddings — [disco.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alembics/disco-diffusion/main/disco.py). No quantitative evaluation of either exists in any source found. Community configs push `clamp_max` to 0.26 to 0.7 — [HuemanInstrument config](https://gist.github.com/HuemanInstrument/c2ab7b821d78d071df5f443be9e95d6d)
- **DSG:** `x_{t−1} = μ_θ(x_t) + r·d_m/‖d_m‖`, with `r = √n·σ_t`, `d_m = d_sample + g_r(d* − d_sample)`, `d* = −√n·σ_t·∇L/‖∇L‖`, and g_r in 0.05 to 0.2 (0.1 for style and text, 0.05 FaceID). FFHQ inpainting at 50 steps, DPS against DPS+DSG: PSNR 22.74 to 30.67, LPIPS 0.314 to 0.176. At 20 steps: 12.08 to 27.53. CelebA FaceID, FreeDoM against +DSG: 0.545 to 0.371, FID 39.61 to 34.29. Text and style results are qualitative only — [DSG](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.03201v4)
- **Shen et al.:** random augmentation `g_t = (1/|𝒯|)Σ_T ∇ℓ(f(T(x̂0)))` with |𝒯| = 10 costs 0.541 to 0.571 s per step. The Polyak step `η‖ε_θ‖/‖g‖²·g` has the combined result of 27.7 against FreeDoM's 25.9 on unconditional ImageNet CLIP. The paper's text holds no per-technique ablation numbers — [Shen et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12404v2)
- **APG** (for CFG, EDM2-S at w=4): FID 10.42 to 6.49 and saturation 0.46 to 0.33. Without projection 6.63, without rescaling 7.93, without momentum 6.85. Negative momentum β in [−0.75, −0.25] — [APG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02416), as recorded in the earlier notes
- **GradCheck:** guiding through x̂0 *plus Adam* took FID from 126.5 to 24.3 and accuracy from 15.6 to 85.9% on a small dataset. Adam alone did not help — [GradCheck](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17399v1), as recorded in the earlier notes
- **DOODL** clips to ±1e-3 with momentum 0.9 on seed optimisation — [DOODL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13703)
- **Adaptive schedules:** EluCD's sine-shaped schedule improves classifier-guided FID 5.57 to 5.24 — [EluCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11311). SAG and UPainting guide only a middle window, or skip the first steps — [SAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.12030v1); [UPainting](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.16031)
- **Spectral:** published frequency work in diffusion targets CFG, not loss gradients. FreCaS applies frequency-aware CFG with separate low and high band strengths, motivated by coarse-to-fine generation. FreeDiff truncates frequencies progressively for editing, warning that early low-frequency guidance misaligns edits — [FreCaS](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18410v2); [FreeDiff](https://mlanthology.org/eccv/2024/wu2024eccv-freediff). No paper found applies low-frequency-first filtering to classifier or CLIP gradients.
- **[measured here]**, all against the default, ± 95% CI (prompt count). Sine schedule −0.006 ± 0.009 (4). Rising schedule −0.011 ± 0.012 (2). Gradient blur σ 2 px −0.021 ± 0.010, 0 of 12 prompts improved. Blur σ 1 px −0.006 ± 0.008. `-gl 0.99` −0.016 ± 0.012 alone and +0.000 ± 0.016 with the ensemble. Also: "Cutout gradients are noisy, but removing the noise changes nothing, because the guidance step is capped" — [README](/home/sam/GitHub/clip-guided-diffusion/README.md); [cutouts_and_gradient_noise.md](../CLIP%20guided%20diffusion%20quality%20improvements/cutouts_and_gradient_noise.md)
- **[measured here, derived]** The repo's cap is FreeDoM's RMS rule with a ceiling at 2 to 4x its rate, and DSG's sphere would change the step radius by about 0.3% at these step sizes — [guidance_formulations.md Q4](../CLIP%20guided%20diffusion%20quality%20improvements/guidance_formulations.md)

### Inferences
- Magnitude control is solved here, since the relative cap already does what `clamp_grad`, FreeDoM normalisation and DSG do. The remaining levers are about *direction*: which x0 the gradient sees (Q4) and whether the guide is noise-aware (Q1). Conditioning tricks act downstream of a direction that, at high noise, carries little information.
- Adam or momentum across steps is the one direction-changing trick with any positive evidence (GradCheck), but that evidence is on a small dataset with a classifier. With the cap in place, Adam's per-pixel normalisation would mainly redistribute the step toward low-gradient pixels. Test it only together with a better x0 path, since GradCheck found it helped only alongside x̂0 guidance.
- A low-frequency-first gradient is already supplied by the U-Net Jacobian at high σ (0.03 of power above 0.25 cycles/pixel at σ 0.997). An explicit spectral filter would be redundant at high noise, and was shown to cost score where it is not (blur at low noise).

### Gaps
- No quantitative evaluation of Disco's `clamp_grad` or `clamp_max` exists in the sources found.
- Shen et al.'s per-technique ablations (random augmentation alone, Polyak alone) are not in the paper's text.
- DSG's text and style guidance results are qualitative only.
- No study of Adam or momentum on CLIP guidance of a pixel diffusion model was found.

## Q6. Does adversarial robustness of the guide matter once x0 is cleaner?

### Takeaway
Yes, by the best indirect evidence: a cleaner, in-support x0 does not stop a guide from being exploited. Xu et al. found that even with consistency-model x0, guidance overfit the guiding segmentation model unless x0 was randomised (τ = 0.2), and held-out models scored better with the noise. Theory (Shen et al.) and the noise-aware classifier results say that smoothing the guide is what limits adversarial gradients. But no paper shows a *robust* guide (FARE, TeCoA, CLIPAG) winning end-to-end once the gradient path improves. GradCheck even found robust plus x̂0 plus Adam worse. The repo's naive FARE/TeCoA test lost. The open bet is noise-awareness (Q1) plus randomisation, not adversarial training.

### Cited Findings
- **Xu et al., Table 5:** segmentation mIoU without against with noise on x0. Model A, the guiding model: 0.57 against 0.51. Model A+DA: 0.43 against 0.54. Model B, held out: 0.31 against 0.34. The authors conclude that without noise the solver "overfits the segmentation model used during DIS." Table 4: CM without noise 0.31 / FID 19.29, with noise 0.34 / FID 18.06 — [Xu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12063)
- **Shen et al.:** noise smoothing makes a bounded loss `C·√(2/(πσ²))`-Lipschitz, so time-independent guides are more prone to adversarial gradients. Random augmentation is their remedy — [Shen et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12404v2)
- **GLIDE** hypothesises that CLIP guidance finds adversarial examples for the evaluation CLIP — [GLIDE §5.2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.10741)
- **Kawar et al.:** adversarially robust *noise-aware* classifier guidance improves FID 2.97 to 2.85 at 128px — [Kawar et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2208.08664)
- **GradCheck:** x̂0 plus Adam helped a non-robust classifier a lot, while adding a robust model to that combination made results worse — [GradCheck](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17399v1)
- **Srinivas et al.:** gradients align with the data manifold at *intermediate* robustness, and excessive robustness degrades them — [Srinivas et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19101v2)
- **[measured here]** FARE4 towers' gradients agree across cutout draws at cosine 0.74 to 0.86, against 0.09 to 0.16 for clean ViTs, with 0.07 to 0.16 of energy at high frequency against 0.43 to 0.74. No paper uses FARE or TeCoA for diffusion guidance. Per this task's brief, the earlier end-to-end test of FARE/TeCoA as drop-in guides lost — [losses_and_ensembles.md §2](../CLIP%20guided%20diffusion%20quality%20improvements/losses_and_ensembles.md)
- **PPAP:** making the guide noise-aware, not adversarially robust, is what turned a useless off-the-shelf guide (38.74) into a useful one (27.86 data-free, 19.98 labelled) on unconditional ADM 256 — [PPAP](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.05973)

### Inferences
- Two distinct failure modes are at play, and they need different fixes. (1) At high noise the guide is *off-distribution*, because x̂0 is a blurry mean. A cleaner x0 (Q4) or a noise-aware guide (Q1) fixes that. (2) At every noise level the guide is *exploitable*. Randomisation (cutouts, τ-noise on x0, augmentation), ensembles or held-out-style judges help with that. A cleaner x0 fixes (1) and leaves (2) alone, as Xu et al.'s overfitting result shows.
- Robust towers lost naively here, probably because their gradients are smooth but semantically weaker (lower clean accuracy), and the repo's cap, ensemble and cutouts already handle exploitability. A robust tower is more likely to pay off as a noise-aware student's *teacher*, or as a cone constraint (DVCE) on the clean tower's gradient, than as the guide itself. That is an inference, and nothing tests it.
- A noise-aware student trained by distillation inherits some smoothness for free, because its inputs are noised (Shen's Lipschitz bound). That is a cheaper route to "robust-ish" gradients than adversarial training, and it targets the high-noise stage where the unconditional model needs help.

### Gaps
- No paper found shows a robust or noise-trained guide beating a standard guide *after* the gradient path is improved (multi-step or CM x0), for CLIP or any text guide.
- The adversarial-robustness level that is best for guidance (Srinivas's intermediate point) has not been measured for CLIP. FARE2 against FARE4 is the easy comparison and remains untested.
- Xu et al.'s Model A+DA column is ambiguous about what "DA" stands for (data augmentation is assumed), so the exact reading of Table 5 should be checked against the paper.
