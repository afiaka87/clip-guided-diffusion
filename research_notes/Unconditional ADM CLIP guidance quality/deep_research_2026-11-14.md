# Deep research, 2026-11-14: novel methods for the unconditional 256 pipeline

Web research only (102 agents, 20 primary sources fetched, 100 claims extracted, 25 put to a 3-vote adversarial check, 24 confirmed, 1 refuted, merged into the 9 findings below). Nothing here was run locally. The question it was given is at the end.

## Summary

The verified evidence gives no training-free method shown to close the gap to SD-Turbo. It supports the project's diagnosis that the one-step x0 estimate is the weak point at high noise and that the missing piece is a learned, condition-aware prior. The best-supported new idea is a DEFT-style side network: a 23M-parameter network trained beside the completely frozen 552M unconditional ADM (this exact checkpoint), with no backward pass through the U-Net in training or sampling. It was shown only on inverse problems, so CLIP-embedding conditioning with LAFITE-style noise is an extrapolation. The cheapest untested sampler change is Perturbed-Attention Guidance in its four-layer unconditional configuration (FID 26.21 to 16.23 on the unconditional ADM); the local negative result was on the class-conditional model. PAG, SAG, TSG and TPG are all measured by FID without CLIP guidance, so their effect on prompt fidelity is unknown. Low-noise Restart, Polyak step sizes with extra augmentations, and flow-map look-ahead are each expected to give small gains or cost too much. No claims survived on reward distillation or alternative guide signals (angles 3 and 4), so this shortlist is incomplete there.

Ranked shortlist (ranking is my judgement from the surviving evidence):
1. DEFT-style frozen-base h-transform network conditioned on a noised CLIP embedding. Alternative or complement to the planned timestep-embedding fine-tune.
2. Four-layer PAG on the unconditional checkpoint, added to the ensemble + recurrence sampler. One extra forward pass per step; SAG, TSG and TPG are fallbacks.
3. Autoguidance using an early snapshot of the planned conditional fine-tune as the weak model. This is an inference; the paper rules out synthetic degradations of a single checkpoint.
4. A low-noise Restart interval (below t=300) to complement the existing high-noise recurrence. Expected gain is small.
5. Polyak step size plus DiffAugment-style colour and mask augmentations. Likely tied, given the local gradient-magnitude results.
6. Distilling a flow map of the ADM for look-ahead guidance. High cost, and the gain over best-of-N in the source is about 1-2 points.

## Findings

### 1. A DEFT-style side network is the best-supported cheap way to add conditioning to this exact checkpoint. DEFT keeps the OpenAI unconditional 256x256 ADM (about 550M parameters) completely frozen and trains a separate 23M-parameter attention U-Net (4.2% of the base) to output an additive correction to the unconditional noise prediction (the h-transform). Training needs only forward passes of the frozen model, and sampling needs no backward pass through either network. That removes the per-step U-Net backward pass that CLIP-gradient guidance needs, which is what makes it fit 12 GB. It has not been shown for text or CLIP conditioning.

- Confidence: medium; vote: 3-0 on both merged claims (20, 21)
- Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01781

Appendix E.1: 'we use a fine-tuning network with 23M parameters ... The pre-trained unconditional score model has 550M parameters'; Sec. 3.1: training 'only requires evaluation of the pre-trained model, without needing to backpropagate through the weights'; Fig. 1: 'During sampling, we do not need to backpropagate through either model'. The paper's checkpoints footnote points to github.com/openai/guided-diffusion, whose only unconditional ImageNet checkpoint is 256x256_diffusion_uncond.pt. Reported training was about 1k ImageNet validation images, 200 epochs, batch 16, 3.9 h for inpainting. Limits: experiments cover only inverse problems (inpainting, super-resolution, HDR, deblurring, CT, protein motifs). The h-network also takes the unconditional x0 estimate and the likelihood gradient with respect to it, so a CLIP version still needs a CLIP backward pass on x0 each step and a frozen-model forward pass. The 12 GB conclusion is an inference; the paper gives no VRAM figure. A compound claim about DEFT's limits and code availability was refuted 1-2, so code availability is not established here. Confidence is medium because the facts are verified but the transfer to CLIP conditioning is extrapolated from a single paper. How it combines: the h-network's correction adds to the frozen model's output, so it can be scaled like classifier-free guidance and still be followed by ensemble CLIP guidance and recurrence. It could replace or precede the planned timestep-embedding fine-tune as a lower-memory first experiment.

### 2. For the caption-free training signal in either the DEFT route or the planned fine-tune, LAFITE's recipe is the supported starting point, but its noise level is known only from code. The recipe takes the normalised CLIP image feature, adds unit-normalised Gaussian noise scaled by xi, and renormalises. The paper never states xi and has no ablation over it. The released code hard-codes a 0.25/0.75 feature-to-noise mix (noise-to-feature ratio 3) for image features and 0.1 for real text features. On MS-COCO 256x256, the fixed-noise variant trained on images only reached FID 18.04 / IS 27.20, against 47.21 / 16.95 for the same architecture trained on machine captions and 26.56 / 22.23 for the learned-noise variant.

- Confidence: medium; vote: 3-0 on both merged claims (22, 23)
- Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13792, https://github.com/drboog/Lafite

Eq. 1: h~ = f_img(x) + xi * eps * ||f_img(x)|| / ||eps||, h' = h~/||h~||; the appendix lists search ranges only for tau, lambda and gamma. Code training/loss.py: aug_level_1 = 0.1, aug_level_2 = 0.75, img_fts*(1-0.75) + unit_noise*0.75 then renormalised. Table 1 numbers verified; paper: 'The simple Lafite G performs the best on this dataset'. Limits: LAFITE is a StyleGAN2 generator, not a diffusion model, and the result is on MS-COCO only, so the noise level for an ADM fine-tune must be tuned and its sensitivity is unknown. The 'paper never states xi' point rests on a summarised read of the ar5iv render plus a search. A follow-up paper (Lafite2, 2210.14124) reportedly analyses the pseudo-versus-real text feature gap but was not checked.

### 3. Perturbed-Attention Guidance in its unconditional configuration is an untested, training-free candidate for this checkpoint. On the unconditional ImageNet 256 ADM it improves FID from 26.21 to 16.23 and IS from 39.70 to 88.53 at scale 1.0 (precision 0.61 to 0.82, recall 0.63 to 0.51), ahead of Self-Attention Guidance (FID 20.08, IS 45.56). The unconditional recipe perturbs four attention layers (input_blocks.14.1, 16.1, 17.1 and middle_block.1), whereas the conditional recipe perturbs only input_blocks.14.1. The project's PAG negative result was on the class-conditional model, so it does not directly test this. The effect on CLIP-guided prompt fidelity is unmeasured.

- Confidence: medium; vote: 2-1 (claim 6), 3-0 (claims 7, 8)
- Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17377, https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.00939

PAG Table 1 (250-step DDPM, 50K samples) and Appendix A.1 layer list verified; SAG Table 1 gives the same baseline row and 20.08 / 5.77 sFID / 45.56 / precision 0.68 / recall 0.59. One verifier vote dissented on the PAG result claim because its original wording said the unconditional gain was 'far larger in relative terms'; that is wrong (FID falls 38% unconditional versus 42% conditional; only the absolute gain, 9.98 versus 4.62 points, is larger) and is dropped here. Limits: all numbers are the authors' own, for unguided sampling scored by FID/IS; scale and layers were selected on FID; recall falls, so part of the gain is a diversity-for-fidelity trade; the paper notes over-saturation at high scales. Cost: one extra U-Net forward pass per step with no extra backward pass, so roughly a third more time per guided step by my estimate and little extra memory. I did not check which layers the project's earlier conditional test perturbed. How it combines: apply the PAG extrapolation to the noise prediction before the CLIP gradient step; it remains usable after a conditioning fine-tune alongside classifier-free guidance.

### 4. Self-guidance gains shrink sharply once another guidance signal is present, so the realistic gain from PAG, SAG or similar methods on top of CLIP guidance or later classifier-free guidance is likely much smaller than the unguided FID numbers suggest. On ADM ImageNet 128, SAG takes FID from 5.91 to 5.11 alone but only from 2.97 to 2.58 on top of classifier guidance; on DiT-XL/2 at 256 it takes classifier-free guidance from 2.27 to 2.16.

- Confidence: high; vote: 3-0 (claim 9)
- Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.00939

SAG Tables 3 and 4 verified: neither 5.91, SAG only 5.11, classifier guidance only 2.97, both 2.58; precision 0.70, 0.72, 0.78, 0.79. Paper: 'we observe additional improvements in FID and precision when using both of them'. The gain on top of other guidance is 0.11 to 0.39 FID against 0.80 unguided. sFID is best with SAG alone (4.09 versus 4.35 with both). The evidence is for a noise-trained class classifier and for classifier-free guidance, not for CLIP-gradient guidance or prompt fidelity.

### 5. Two other condition-free self-guidance methods apply in principle to the ADM but have evidence only on latent models. Time-step guidance (TSG) adds zero-mean Gaussian noise to the timestep embedding and extrapolates away from the perturbed prediction, at two forward passes per step; it lowered unconditional FID from 48.67 to 29.03 on DiT-XL/2 and from 69.38 to 56.65 on Stable Diffusion. Token Perturbation Guidance (TPG) shuffles tokens in hidden states; on unconditional SDXL it lowered FID from 124.04 to 69.31 against PAG 98.83 and SEG 82.64, and on SD 2.1 from 25.24 to 16.69 against PAG 21.30 and SEG 20.98. TPG code is public. Neither was tested on a pixel-space model, on ImageNet ADM, or with CLIP or classifier guidance.

- Confidence: medium; vote: 3-0 on all four merged claims (13, 14, 15, 16)
- Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02687, https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10036, https://github.com/TaatiTeam/Token-Perturbation-Guidance

TSG: perturbation t_emb + s*t^alpha*n and Table 3 verified (DiT precision 0.48 to 0.69, recall 0.59 to 0.55; 10k samples); the authors say they did not grid-search s and alpha and that too much noise hurts. TPG: Tables 1 and 2 verified (30k samples, MS-COCO 2014 reference, scale 3.0); 'unconditional' there means text-to-image models run without a prompt; on conditional SDXL, TPG is worse than classifier-free guidance (FID 17.77 versus 12.79, CLIP score 30.15 versus 32.03); the authors state 'the guidance term may remain limited in extreme out-of-distribution scenarios'. TPG beating PAG on latent models is the only reason to prefer it over PAG here; PAG is the one with ADM evidence. TSG is the simplest to try on the ADM because it only touches the timestep embedding, but its hyperparameters would need re-tuning. Confidence is medium because the numbers are verified but transfer is untested.

### 6. Autoguidance works without conditioning but cannot be applied to the released checkpoint as it stands. On unconditional ImageNet-512 with EDM2-S it lowers FID from 11.67 to 3.86, using a separately trained guiding model one size smaller with 1/16 of the training iterations (about 3.6% extra training cost). The paper reports that synthetic degradations of the main model 'did not work at all'. No smaller or earlier snapshot of the unconditional 256 ADM is public.

- Confidence: high; vote: 3-0 on all three merged claims (10, 11, 12)
- Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02507

Section 5 and Table 1 verified (FD-DINOv2 209.53 to 90.39); 'One limitation of autoguidance is the need to train a separate guiding model'; 'deriving the guiding model from the main model using synthetic degradations did not work at all'. The unconditional result is on EDM2 latent diffusion, not pixel-space ADM, and measures FID, not prompt fidelity. Two points go beyond the paper. First, a verifier's search surfaced later training-free weak-model methods (S2-Guidance 2508.12880, In-situ Autoguidance 2510.17136, Self-Guidance 2412.05827, masked sliding window guidance) that reportedly help; those papers were not opened, so their numbers are unverified. Second, my inference: an early snapshot of the planned conditional fine-tune is a less-trained model on the same task and data, which is the kind of guide the paper's ablation favours, so the fine-tune would supply an autoguidance guide at no extra training cost. This is untested.

### 7. Flow-map look-ahead addresses the project's exact diagnosis, but it needs a distilled flow map of the ADM and its measured edge over best-of-N is small. The FMTT paper attributes the weakness of denoiser look-ahead to 'heavily blurred predictions at early, high-noise timesteps, which fall far off the data manifold'. With a flow-map look-ahead it scored 74.86 on UniGenBench++ at 3,840 function evaluations, against 73.75 for best-of-N and 72.90 for denoiser look-ahead at 11,520 each (base FLUX.1 dev 61.78). On GenEval it reached 0.79 against 0.75 for best-of-N at similar compute, with reward gradients adding only 'a further (small) improvement'.

- Confidence: medium; vote: 3-0 on all three merged claims (3, 4, 5)
- Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22688

Tables 1 and 2 verified; a 2-step flow-map row scores 75.55 at 6,720 evaluations, so the best margin over best-of-N is about 1.8 points. The look-ahead ablation puts the flow map's own contribution at about 0.04 GenEval (0.79 versus 0.75 for 1-step denoiser or 4-step diffusion look-ahead). The flow map was distilled from FLUX.1 dev in about 4 hours on 8 A100s; none exists for the unconditional ADM, and all evidence is on a text-conditioned latent flow model with 256 particles. The paper flags reward hacking. That most of the gain comes from search rather than gradients is an inference from the table, not a clean ablation. The project's own result that a 3-step no-grad x0 rollout lost is a weak prior against look-aheads, though a distilled flow map is a different estimator. Thousands of function evaluations per image is far beyond the current 30-50 s budget, and by my estimate distilling a 552M pixel model on one 12 GB GPU would take most of the available days. Confidence is medium because the source is a single self-reported paper with no replication.

### 8. The closest published benchmark to this project suggests the remaining training-free headroom is small and that multi-sample Monte Carlo estimators are not the answer. On unconditional ImageNet diffusion guided by CLIP-B/16 text and judged by CLIP-L/14, random augmentation plus Polyak step sizes scored 27.7 against FreeDoM 25.9, Universal Guidance 25.7, MPGD-Z 25.1 and LGD-MC 24.3 (the weakest). The same paper argues that training-free loss guidance is more prone to adversarial gradients and converges more slowly than guidance with a noise-trained classifier. Its augmentation fix is essentially what cutouts already do.

- Confidence: medium; vote: 3-0 on all three merged claims (0, 1, 2)
- Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12404, https://github.com/BIGKnight/Understanding-Training-free-Diffusion-Guidance

Table 2 verified; 100 DDIM steps in the general setup; FreeDoM and MPGD-Z baselines use time-travel. Sec. 4.1 uses 10 DiffAugment transformations (translation, resizing, colour adjustment, masking cutout) and says they 'introduce perturbations within a lower-dimensional latent space, thus requiring fewer samples'. Two parts are not already in the pipeline: colour and masking augmentations on the guide's input, and the Polyak step size. The project found gradient-magnitude tricks tied, so the 1.8-point margin may not carry over. One number per method, no confidence intervals, no independent replication, and the exact checkpoint was not confirmed as the 256 ADM. The theory rests on smoothness assumptions and is not a measurement on this pipeline; it does support a noise-aware or learned conditional signal over more sampler tricks. Code is public. Confidence is medium because the facts are verified but relevance to a pipeline that already has cutouts, ensembles and recurrence is uncertain.

### 9. A low-noise Restart interval is a materially different variant of the existing recurrence, but its expected gain on prompt fidelity is small. Restart repeatedly re-noises from t_min to t_max and re-solves the backward ODE K times, typically late in sampling (for example sigma 0.1 to 2 or 3 with K=2 and 10 steps on Stable Diffusion, 0.06 to 0.30 on CIFAR-10). On Stable Diffusion v1.5 its gain is FID at a matched CLIP score: at guidance 8, FID 18.40 / CLIP 0.3228 / aesthetic 5.41 against DDIM-50 at 19.83 / 0.3206 / 5.37. The project's recurrence runs only above t=300, so the region below it (roughly sigma 0.1 to 1.2) is uncovered.

- Confidence: medium; vote: 3-0 on all three merged claims (17, 18, 19)
- Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14878

Abstract: 'ODE-samplers involve smaller discretization errors while stochasticity in SDE contracts accumulated errors'; Sec. 4.3: 'we typically position the Restart interval towards the end of the simulation, where the accumulated error is larger'. Appendix Tables 6-9 verified: Restart's CLIP gain over DDIM is +0.002 to +0.008, and DDPM beats it on CLIP score at guidance 2, 3 and 5. FID first improves then worsens as K grows; configurations were hand-picked from 55 sets. Corrections from verification: Restart was tested on unconditional CIFAR-10 (not only class-conditional models), and it is not exclusively low-noise (ImageNet 64 configurations include a high-noise interval), so 'opposite placement' is overstated. The sigma 1.2 conversion of t=300 is a verifier's calculation, not from the source. No CLIP-gradient or ImageNet 256 ADM experiment exists, and the pipeline already uses an SDE sampler, which the paper treats as the other route to the same error contraction. That the gain would be well below the +0.018 measured for recurrence is an extrapolation across different settings and judges.

## Caveats

- Coverage is partial. The 24 surviving claims cover self-guidance, Restart, flow-map look-ahead, training-free guidance theory, DEFT and LAFITE. Nothing survived on angle 3 (distilling guidance, score distillation from a text-to-image teacher, reward fine-tuning, noise-aware CLIP) or angle 4 (VQAScore, PickScore, HPS, ImageReward, DINOv2 or discriminator guides), and almost nothing on REPA, loss weighting, IP-Adapter-style designs or reports of fine-tuning the ADM checkpoints. Their absence here is not evidence that they do not help.
- No source measures what the project measures. Apart from one CLIP-L/14-judged table (2403.12404), every result is FID, IS or a benchmark on a different model. None reports held-out CLIP scores for CLIP-guided sampling from the unconditional 256 ADM, so I give no numeric expected gains; any number would be a guess.
- Almost all evidence is single-paper and author-reported, with no independent replication checked. Several verifications used summarising fetches or read only part of the HTML, and most ran no search for contradicting work.
- The ranking and the 'how it combines' notes are my inferences, not findings from the sources. The same holds for the ideas of training a DEFT network on noised CLIP embeddings and of using an early fine-tune snapshot as an autoguidance guide.
- PAG's result claim passed 2-1 because of a wrong relative-gain sentence, which is corrected here. A DEFT claim about its limits and code availability was refuted 1-2, so whether DEFT's code is public is not established.
- LAFITE's noise level comes only from hard-coded values in its repository, and its result is for a GAN on MS-COCO.
- Time sensitivity: the training-free self-guidance field is moving fast (S2-Guidance, In-situ Autoguidance, masked sliding window guidance were surfaced but not read), and FMTT was revised in November 2026. This is web research only; nothing was run locally.

## Open questions

- Does four-layer PAG (or TSG, which only perturbs the timestep embedding) raise held-out CLIP scores when combined with ensemble CLIP guidance and recurrence on the unconditional ADM, or does it only improve realism and aesthetic rating, as SAG's shrinking gain under classifier guidance suggests?
- Can a DEFT-style 23M side network learn CLIP-embedding conditioning from caption-free LAION images with LAFITE noise, and how does it compare with the planned timestep-embedding fine-tune at equal GPU-days on 12 GB? What noise level bridges the text-image gap for a diffusion model, given that LAFITE's only documented value is the code's 0.75 mix?
- Does an early snapshot of the conditional fine-tune work as an autoguidance guide for the finished one, and do the unread training-free weak-model methods (S2-Guidance, In-situ Autoguidance, masked sliding window guidance) hold up on a pixel-space eps-prediction U-Net?
- What does the evidence say for the angles with no surviving claims: noise-aware CLIP or timestep-conditioned reward models, score distillation from SD-Turbo as a sampling-time signal, reward fine-tuning at 12 GB, and human-preference or VQA guides judged by held-out models?

## What was run afterwards (2026-11-14 to 15)

Unlike everything above, this was measured locally, on the RTX 4070. The numbers, arms and blind sheets are in `reports/evidence/uncond/README.md`, sections "Composition from a thumbnail model" and "Perturbed-attention guidance, four layers".

- Shortlist item 2, four-layer PAG at scale 1: run. Alone it is a trade, as finding 4 warned: against the `-uncond` default the held-out CLIP judges fall by 0.0286 ± 0.0181 and the aesthetic rating rises by 0.29 ± 0.33, and a blind judge preferred it in 16 of 24 pairs. That answers the first open question: PAG raises realism and the rating, not agreement with the prompt.
- Finding 8's argument for a noise-trained guide, and the fourth open question: run, and it is the largest gain here that needs no text-to-image model. OpenAI's CLIP of noisy 64×64 images (released with GLIDE) guides the 64px average-pooled thumbnail of the noisy image, on top of the usual CLIP guidance. Against the default on 66 confirmation prompts: +0.0159 ± 0.0078 on the CLIP judges, +0.34 ± 0.21 on PickScore, and a blind judge preferred it in 50 of 66 pairs, for 2 s more an image. GLIDE's, LAIONIDE's and Karlo's 64px text-conditional models guide the thumbnail the same way and gain as much or more, but they are text-to-image models, and with Karlo the result only ties Karlo's own pipeline.
- The two together: PAG on top of that thumbnail guidance raises PickScore, HPS and the rating on both prompt sets and lowers agreement by about 0.01, for a third more time. A blind judge preferred it to thumbnail guidance alone in 54 of 90 pairs (p 0.07).
- Not run: the DEFT side network (1), autoguidance (3), a low-noise Restart interval (4), Polyak step sizes with colour and mask augmentations (5), a distilled flow map (6), and TSG, SAG and TPG (finding 5). Items 1 and 3 need training, and 1 remains the best-supported direction for it.

## Refuted in verification

- DEFT's evidence does not cover text, class or CLIP conditioning: it is evaluated only on inverse problems (inpainting, super-resolution, HDR, deblurring, phase retrieval, CT) and protein motif scaffolding, and it needs a dataset of paired clean samples and conditions. The data-free reward/stochastic-control variant (tilting by a reward r(x0), the form a CLIP score would take) is stated to be infeasible at image scale because the whole sampling chain must be held in memory; adjoint, VarGrad, trajectory-balance and truncated-backprop alternatives are only discussed. Transfer to CLIP-embedding conditioning of ADM is therefore an extrapolation, and the arXiv abstract page links no code repository. (vote 1-2, https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01781)

## The question

Research question: what advanced or novel methods (2022-2026 literature, code and practitioner evidence) could materially improve the image quality and prompt fidelity of CLIP-guided sampling from OpenAI's UNCONDITIONAL 256x256 ImageNet ADM checkpoint (guided-diffusion `256x256_diffusion_uncond.pt`: 552M-parameter pixel-space U-Net, epsilon prediction with learned sigma, linear 1000-step schedule, no class or text input), on a single RTX 4070 12 GB with days (not weeks) of compute? This is web research only: do not run local code, benchmarks or anything on a GPU.

Current pipeline (github.com/afiaka87/clip-guided-diffusion): text-to-image by guiding the unconditional model with the gradient of a CLIP loss taken on random cutouts of the one-step x0 prediction and backpropagated through the U-Net; guide is an ensemble of OpenAI ViT-B/32 + PE-Core-B-16; DPM-Solver++(2M) SDE, 60 steps in uniform logSNR, guidance step capped relative to the x0 change (rate 0.08), guidance window t in [0.1, 1.0], TV and range losses, self-recurrence (re-noise and redo each guided step twice above t=300). About 30-50 s per image.

Measured so far on 12 held-out prompts x 3 seeds, judged by held-out CLIP models (ViT-B/16, DFN ViT-H-14, SigLIP2 ViT-L): baseline ViT-B/32 guide 0.299/0.274/0.097; ensemble guide 0.323/0.314/0.124; ensemble + recurrence 0.338/0.332/0.144; SD-Turbo with CLIP noise optimisation 0.352/0.383/0.190 (target). Aesthetic-predictor rating 5.5 vs SD-Turbo's 7.1. Already tested and settled, so do NOT re-propose unless there is a materially different variant: guide ensembles (helped, +0.030), recurrence/time-travel (helped, +0.018), more sampler steps (tied recurrence), best-of-N selection (helped, +0.024 at 4x cost), Feynman-Kac steering and progressive pruning (only tied best-of-N), a 3-step no-grad x0 rollout with one-step Jacobian at high noise (lost), gradient-magnitude tricks (DSG spherical steps, clamp_grad, gradient blur, guidance schedules: all tied), adversarially robust CLIP towers as guides (lost), perturbed-attention guidance on the class-conditional model (lost), Disco Diffusion's secondary model (tied), SDEdit refinement through SD-Turbo (helps but leaves ADM). Diagnosis: at sigma > 0.9 the unconditional x0 prediction is a grey blur, so CLIP's gradient carries little layout information, and most of SD-Turbo's lead is its text-conditioned prior rather than guidance. Planned but unmeasured: fine-tuning a zero-initialised projection of a CLIP embedding into the U-Net's timestep embedding (plus the ResBlock embedding layers and attention), trained on 5.6M LAION-aesthetic 256px image-caption pairs with LAFITE-style embedding noise and 10% condition dropout, then classifier-free guidance plus CLIP guidance.

Find the strongest evidence-backed ideas in these five angles, with concrete numbers, compute/memory costs, and whether code or weights are public:
1.
