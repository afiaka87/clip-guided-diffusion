# Disco Diffusion practice, cascades and refinement, and evaluation for OpenAI's unconditional ADM models

Status: 2026-11-13. Labels used below: **[code]** read directly from source code, **[published]** paper or model card, **[community]** settings or claims posted by practitioners with no measurement behind them, **[measured here]** numbers from this repo's saved evidence, **[reanalysis]** new arithmetic on this repo's saved per-image scores, done for these notes. The earlier notes in `research_notes/CLIP guided diffusion quality improvements/` (`refinement_pipelines.md`, `practitioner_tricks_and_novel_ideas.md`) already cover the ADM upsampler flags and sizes, the secondary model and Disco's cutout code in detail. They are cited here rather than repeated. Most local numbers come from the class-conditional 128 px pipeline, not the unconditional 256 model, and that is flagged wherever it matters.

## 1. Disco Diffusion era settings for the 256/512 unconditional models: what they were, why, and which have evidence

### Takeaway
Almost nothing in Disco's settings was ever ablated. The defaults (250 DDIM steps, guidance 5000, range 150, TV 0, cutn_batches 4, overview-to-inner cut schedule, clamp_max 0.05, eta 0.8, secondary model on, ViT-B/32+ViT-B/16+RN50) are code facts, but the evidence behind them is image grids and Discord consensus. Some of those grids, such as KaliYuga's CLIP model comparison and the EZ Charts wiki, are no longer reachable. The only controlled numbers that exist are this repo's. They show that ensembles help, cutout layout and Disco's cut mix do not, the secondary model ties at 256, and TV 0 trends worse than 150. On the unconditional 256 model the only local comparison is 1000 ancestral steps against 60 DPM-Solver steps, 0.331 against 0.316 on 6 prompts, with an interval that spans zero.

### Cited Findings

**Lineage: how the unconditional 256 notebook started, and what changed**
- Crowson's first CLIP-guided notebook for `256x256_diffusion_uncond.pt` (as imported into this repo on 2021-07-20) used ViT-B/16, `clip_guidance_scale = 2750` and `timestep_respacing '500'` with ancestral `p_sample_loop_progressive`. CLIP did not see a predicted x0. It saw the *noisy* x_t Gaussian-blurred with `sigma = min(24, 0.25*sqrt(1-ᾱ)/sqrt(ᾱ))`, as a single full frame with ±8/224 translation and `RandomAutocontrast(p=0.1)`, and `clip_denoised=True` [code] — [this repo, first commit 86cc450, `clip-guided-diffusion.ipynb`](https://github.com/afiaka87/clip-guided-diffusion/commit/86cc450) (read locally with `git show`)
- The later "HQ" form, which Disco inherited, blends the model's x0 prediction with the noisy sample, `x_in = pred_xstart*fac + x*(1-fac)` with `fac = sqrt_one_minus_alphas_cumprod[t]`, and applies random cutouts (`size = rand**cut_pow*(max-min)+min`) plus TV, range and LPIPS init losses. The EleutherAI Space's own example uses guidance 1000, TV 150, range 50, 90 respaced steps and 32 cuts. Its slider defaults are guidance 600, TV 0, range 0, 50 steps and 32 cuts. Its comments say `skip_timesteps` "needs to be between approx. 200 and 500 when using an init image" and that an init_scale of 1000 is "a good value" [code] — [EleutherAI/clip-guided-diffusion app.py](https://huggingface.co/spaces/EleutherAI/clip-guided-diffusion/blob/main/app.py)
- Disco's credits say Daniel Russell tuned the original for "quick generations in 15-100 timesteps rather than 1000", and that loading several CLIP models "may greatly improve accuracy" [code/community] — [MMagic Disco README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/open-mmlab/mmagic@main/configs/disco_diffusion/README.md)

**Disco v5.6/5.7 code defaults** (all [code], [disco.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alembics/disco-diffusion/main/disco.py))
- `steps = 250` (menu 25 to 1000), `timestep_respacing = ddim{steps}`, `diffusion_steps = (1000//steps)*steps`, `diffusion_sampling_mode = 'ddim'` with `'plms'` as the alternative, `eta = 0.8`, `skip_steps = 10`, `init_scale = 1000`.
- `clip_guidance_scale = 5000`, `tv_scale = 0`, `range_scale = 150`, `sat_scale = 0`, `cutn_batches = 4`, `skip_augs = False`, `clamp_grad = True`, `clamp_max = 0.05` (RMS cap: `grad*magnitude.clamp(max=clamp_max)/magnitude`, NaN steps are zeroed), `randomize_class = True`, `clip_denoised = False`, `fuzzy_prompt = False`, `rand_mag = 0.05`, `perlin_init = False`, `perlin_mode = 'mixed'`.
- Cut schedules indexed over 1000: `cut_overview "[12]*400+[4]*600"`, `cut_innercut "[4]*400+[12]*600"`, `cut_ic_pow "[1]*1000"`, `cut_icgray_p "[0.2]*400+[0]*600"`. The notebook's description: "Overview cuts see the entire image and are good for early structure, innercuts are your standard cutn."
- CLIP: ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16 and RN50 on by default. ViT-L/14, ViT-L/14@336, RN101, RN50x4/x16/x64 and OpenCLIP models are optional.
- Default model `512x512_diffusion_uncond_finetune_008100`, with the secondary model on and `use_checkpoint = True`.
- **Output size is not the training size.** `width_height_for_256x256_models = [512, 448]` and `width_height_for_512x512_models = [1280, 768]`, so Disco's default renders the 256 models at 3.5 times and the 512 models at 3.75 times their training pixel count.
- The settings table inside disco.py is headed "Defaults are heavily outdated". It lists guidance 1000, TV 150, range 150, cutn 16, cutn_batches 2, eta 0.5, rand_mag 0.1 and default CLIP models "ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, RN50x4", described as "Typically the more, the better but they all come at a hefty VRAM cost".
- `randomize_class` has nothing to act on with the unconditional checkpoints. Disco calls the sampler with `model_kwargs={}`, and the class is only randomised in `y`.
- `fuzzy_prompt` adds `randn*rand_mag` to each text embedding and then calls `.clamp(0,1)` on the *embedding*. That zeroes every negative component of the CLIP target, so the "fuzzy" prompts are not small perturbations of the prompt.
- Symmetry (v5.3, nshepperd/huemin/cut_pow) overwrites the right or bottom half of the *noisy sample* with the mirrored left or top half, at fractions `transformation_steps` of the run (default `[0.01]`, i.e. once, near the start).
- The KaliYuga models get their own cut schedules: pixel art and Pulp SciFi `overview [15]*200+[12]*200+[6]*100+[4]*100+[2]*200+[0]*200`, `innercut [1]*200+[4]*200+[8]*200+[10]*400`, `cut_ic_pow` 12 falling to 10, `cut_icgray_p` 0.87 falling to 0 by step 600. Watercolor: `overview [14]*200+[12]*200+[4]*400+[0]*200`, `icgray 0.7 to 0`. The notebook comment: for cut_ic_pow, "Values between 1 and 100 all work", pointing to the EZ Charts wiki.

**This repo's controlled numbers on Disco knobs** (class-conditional ADM unless stated)
- Unconditional 256: 1000 ancestral steps scored 0.331 against 0.316 for 60 DPM-Solver++ steps on ViT-B/16, 6 prompts, difference 95% CI −0.040 to +0.014. The original won on 5 of 6 prompts at 18x the time [measured here] — [README, Speed](../../README.md)
- Cutouts: on 2 seeds, Disco's whole-frame-plus-small-crops mix lost (−0.017 ± 0.010), as did reflect padding (−0.022 ± 0.023) and sizes that change during sampling (−0.007 to −0.012). Stratified, mirrored and whole-frame-added layouts tied. On SD-Turbo 512, eleven layouts stayed within 0.007 of 4 random cuts [measured here] — [README](../../README.md)
- Secondary model in place of backprop through the U-Net: at 256 it tied on three judges (−0.011 to +0.008) and cut VRAM from 5.3 to 1.9 GiB. At 512 it trended lower (−0.012 to −0.015). At 128 on 4 seeds it lost (−0.019 pooled) [measured here] — [refinement_pipelines.md §2](../CLIP%20guided%20diffusion%20quality%20improvements/refinement_pipelines.md); [report](../../reports/CLIP%20guided%20diffusion%20quality%20improvements.md)
- Ensembles: ViT-B/32 + PE-Core-B-16 gained +0.0188 ± 0.015 pooled over 3 held-out judges (10 of 12 prompts, 4 seeds), and the gain repeated at 256 and 512 [measured here] — [report](../../reports/CLIP%20guided%20diffusion%20quality%20improvements.md)
- Disco's own default ensemble, ViT-B/32 + ViT-B/16 + RN50, against ViT-B/32 alone (128 px, seed 0, 12 prompts, ±1.96 SE): ViT-B/16 +0.008 ± 0.013 (not a held-out judge here, because B/16 guided), ViT-H +0.011 ± 0.032, SigLIP2 +0.030 ± 0.019. The same pilot's B/32+PE ensemble: +0.012 ± 0.015, +0.022 ± 0.023, +0.037 ± 0.024 [reanalysis] — [judge_scores.json](../../reports/evidence/scores/judge_scores.json)
- TV, same pilot: TV 0 against 150 gave −0.013 ± 0.016, −0.019 ± 0.033, −0.008 ± 0.018, and TV 50 against 150 gave +0.003, +0.002, +0.015 ± 0.009. Disco's default of TV 0 trends worse on this pipeline, on one seed [reanalysis] — [judge_scores.json](../../reports/evidence/scores/judge_scores.json)
- Dynamic thresholding lost (−0.0189 ± 0.014), and PAG lost [measured here] — [report](../../reports/CLIP%20guided%20diffusion%20quality%20improvements.md)

### Inferences
- **Evidence tier, best to worst.** (a) Controlled here: ensembles help; cutout count and layout, Disco's cut mix, dynamic thresholding and PAG do not; secondary model ties at 256; TV 150 beats TV 0 weakly. (b) Published: GLIDE's result that CLIP guidance loses to CFG with humans, and that a 64 px noised-CLIP base plus upsampler matches the 256/512 notebooks. (c) Community grids, now unreachable: CLIP model choice (KaliYuga), cut_ic_pow and other EZ Charts. (d) Folklore: clamp_max values, very high sat/TV, eta 0.8-0.9 "important", "no secondary model, important", perlin init, fuzzy prompts, symmetry, PLMS against DDIM, cutn_batches.
- Disco's guidance scales do not port. clamp_max caps the RMS of the gradient per step, and its correct value depends on the step count and sampler. This repo caps guidance relative to the noise level, which already removes that dependence.
- `fuzzy_prompt` is effectively a bug, because of the clamp to [0,1], and `randomize_class` is a no-op on unconditional checkpoints. Neither is worth porting.
- Disco's default output sizes, 512x448 from a 256 model and 1280x768 from a 512 model, mean its famous look partly comes from running the U-Net far beyond its training resolution. That is a likely source of the repeated motifs and "texture soup" people associate with it (see question 2), and a reason not to copy its size defaults when chasing 256 quality.
- The one unconditional-model gap that matters is the step count: 1000 steps against 60 steps trended in favour of 1000 (−0.015, 5 of 6 prompts). An unconditional 256 test of 120 and 250 steps at the same guidance rate, on enough prompts (see question 5), is the cheapest Disco-era claim left to settle.

### Gaps
- No primary source compares any Disco knob under fixed seeds with a metric. The EZ Charts wiki and Zippy's cheatsheet are offline (410/404), the KaliYuga comparison is truncated, and Reddit and Discord archives were not reachable.
- No source compares the 256 unconditional model against the 512 fine-tune under CLIP guidance at matched settings.
- PLMS against DDIM for CLIP guidance has no measurement anywhere I found.

## 2. Failure modes of the unconditional models under CLIP guidance, and the fixes practitioners used

### Takeaway
The documented failure modes are: faces, which the model card itself warns about and which drove the portrait fine-tune; composition, which CLIP must supply alone without a class (measured here as the unconditional 256 model losing to the slow sampler); and adversarial texture, where CLIP is satisfied by patterns humans do not read as the object (GLIDE's human study). The "ImageNet dog" bias and repeated objects are widely repeated but I found no measurement of them. The practical fixes were inits that supply layout, symmetry, fine-tuned priors and best-of-N selection. Only selection and ensembles have numbers.

### Cited Findings
- The 512 unconditional fine-tune "sometimes produce[s] highly unrealistic outputs, particularly when generating images containing human faces", which the card attributes to ImageNet's emphasis on non-human objects. It was fine-tuned for 8,100 steps from OpenAI's 512 class-conditional model "to enable better guidance by CLIP (or any other non-ImageNet classifier)". MIT [published] — [lowlevelware/512x512_diffusion_unconditional_ImageNet](https://huggingface.co/lowlevelware/512x512_diffusion_unconditional_ImageNet)
- Disco v5.6 added `portrait_generator_v001`, "a 512x512 diffusion model trained on faces", which is the community's answer to the face problem [code] — [disco-diffusion README](https://github.com/alembics/disco-diffusion)
- Without a class, "CLIP has to set the composition too", and the unconditional 256 model at 60 steps scored behind the 1000-step original on 5 of 6 prompts [measured here] — [README](../../README.md)
- Prompt fit is set by whatever produces the layout. On the class-conditional pipeline, "A pencil sketch of an old man with a beard" became a pen at 64 and 128, and no upscaler or SD-Turbo refinement fixed it. The refinement "inherits ADM's errors" [measured here] — [report](../../reports/CLIP%20guided%20diffusion%20quality%20improvements.md)
- GLIDE's human study preferred classifier-free guidance over CLIP guidance on both photorealism and caption match [published] — [GLIDE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10741)
- Centipede Diffusion feeds a Latent Diffusion output to Disco as an init: "LL creates a cohesive overall shape. Then DD adds the details". The author rerolls cheap LD layouts before spending Disco time on them [community] — [Centipede README](https://github.com/Zalring/Centipede_Diffusion/blob/main/README.md)
- Disco's symmetry transform mirrors half of the noisy sample once, early in the run, to force a symmetric layout [code] — [disco.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alembics/disco-diffusion/main/disco.py)
- Best-of-N on held-out judges was the robust win on ADM: best of 4 +0.0154 ± 0.0070 (11 of 12 prompts), +0.0245 ± 0.0114 with the ensemble. Best of 8 seeds at 64 px gained +0.020 to +0.047 on held-out judges [measured here] — [report](../../reports/CLIP%20guided%20diffusion%20quality%20improvements.md)
- DiffusionCLIP notes that diffusion models trained on ImageNet and similar datasets can produce biased results, and that CLIP-based manipulation carries model biases [published, general] — [DiffusionCLIP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.02711)

### Inferences
- Composition is the binding failure at 256, and it is decided in the first ~20-30% of the schedule. Fixes that act there are the ones worth testing on the unconditional model: best-of-N chosen early (score partial x0 predictions, as the Centipede author did by eye), a layout init from a cheaper model followed by `-init -skip`, or a class-conditional 64/128 pass whose output becomes the unconditional model's init (see question 4).
- Rendering above the training size (Disco's 512x448 from a 256 model) is the most plausible cause of repeated objects. A pixel U-Net with a 256 receptive field tiles its content when asked to fill a larger canvas. This repo already stays at 256 and 512, so that failure mode should not appear here unless the sizes are changed.
- Faces will remain bad on the ImageNet unconditional models whatever the guidance does. Only a face-trained prior (portrait_generator_v001, 512, unknown data) or a modern backbone fixes them.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative study of dog or animal bias, object repetition or "texture soup" in CLIP-guided ADM outputs. These are widely asserted but undocumented.
- No source measured whether symmetry, perlin init or init images improve composition scores.

## 3. Community fine-tunes of the unconditional models: how they were trained, and whether curated data helped

### Takeaway
Most of the famous Disco "fine-tunes" are not fine-tunes of OpenAI's 256 unconditional model. KaliYuga's six models share a smaller architecture (128 channels, attention only at 16, 1 head, no scale-shift norm, 454.9 MB, about 114M parameters at fp32) that matches no OpenAI release, so they were trained from scratch. Each used a few thousand curated images (about 4,100 for pixel art), with improved-diffusion on Colab, because the 553M-parameter OpenAI model did not fit a Colab GPU even at batch 1. The portrait model is also a small 128-channel model, at 512. True fine-tunes of the full architecture exist (the 512 unconditional model after 8,100 steps, danbooru2021 trained on 8x A100 80 GB, ukiyo-e), but nobody published a comparison showing that fine-tuning improved CLIP-guided results. The evidence is showcase images only.

### Cited Findings
- Disco's built-in model list: `256x256_diffusion_uncond`, `512x512_diffusion_uncond_finetune_008100`, `portrait_generator_v001`, `pixelartdiffusion_expanded`, `pixel_art_diffusion_hard_256`, `pixel_art_diffusion_soft_256`, `pixelartdiffusion4k`, `watercolordiffusion_2`, `watercolordiffusion`, `PulpSciFiDiffusion`, plus `custom`, with Hugging Face URLs and SHA-256 hashes [code] — [disco.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alembics/disco-diffusion/main/disco.py)
- Architectures in Disco's loader [code] — [disco.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alembics/disco-diffusion/main/disco.py):
  - OpenAI 256 unconditional and the 512 fine-tune: attention 32,16,8, 256 channels, 64 head channels, 2 res blocks, resblock_updown, scale-shift norm, linear schedule.
  - `portrait_generator_v001`: 512 px, attention 32,16,8, **128 channels**, 4 heads, scale-shift norm. File `portrait_generator_v001_ema_0.9999_1MM.pt`.
  - Every KaliYuga model ("else: # E.g. A model finetuned by KaliYuga"): 256 px, **attention 16 only, 128 channels, 1 head, no resblock_updown, no scale-shift norm**, `timestep_respacing 'ddim100'`.
- All six KaliYuga checkpoints are exactly 454,885,741 bytes. Created on the Hub: hard/soft 2022-06-01, 4k 2022-06-06, PADexpanded 2022-06-19, PulpSciFi 2022-06-20, watercolor_2 2022-07-11. The local `256x256_diffusion_uncond.pt` is 2,211,383,297 bytes, about 553M parameters at fp32 [measured here] — [HF API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/KaliYuga/pixelartdiffusion4k?blobs=true); local `~/.cache/clip-guided-diffusion/`
- `pixelartdiffusion4k`: "Unconditional 256x256 Diffusion model trained on ~4100 hand-picked pixel art pieces", cc-by-3.0, and tools using it must credit KaliYuga. The other KaliYuga cards contain only a license line (cc-by-4.0, or "cc" for the hard model) [published] — [model card](https://huggingface.co/KaliYuga/pixelartdiffusion4k/blob/main/README.md)
- KaliYuga wrote a guide on building an image dataset and training "your own unconditional diffusion model with minimal coding" for CLIP-guided diffusion. Only the title is reachable [community] — [Artvy listing](https://artvy.ai/resource/training-your-own-unconditional-diffusion-model)
- A May 2022 Patreon post by Sxela (Alex Spirin), on tuning an OpenAI diffusion model for better faces with Disco: it used OpenAI's improved-diffusion repo (guided-diffusion also works), said the 256x256 model from vanilla Disco "was too large to fit into the Colab GPU even with a batch of 1", and noted that only images are needed because the generator is unconditional. Seen only as a search summary, because the page returned 403 [community] — [Patreon post 66246423](https://www.patreon.com/posts/66246423)
- Ukiyo-e Portrait Diffusion: 5,000+ ukiyo-e portraits from Justin Pinkney's dataset at 256x256, trained with Alex Spirin's fine-tuning notebook, run from a Disco 5.6 fork. No base, step count or hardware given, and no comparison [community] — [avantcontra/ukiyoe-portrait-diffusion](https://github.com/avantcontra/ukiyoe-portrait-diffusion)
- danbooru2021 256 px: an unconditional guided-diffusion checkpoint with the *same* flags as OpenAI's 256 model (256 channels, 64 head channels, attention 32,16,8), trained on about 4.8M images for about 22 epochs on 8x A100 80 GB (`ema_0.9999_940000.pt`), MIT, usable in Disco. Whether it started from OpenAI's weights is not stated [published] — [naclbit/danbooru2021_256px_guided_diffusion](https://huggingface.co/naclbit/danbooru2021_256px_guided_diffusion)
- The 512 unconditional model is a fine-tune of OpenAI's 512 class-conditional model for 8,100 steps. Batch size and hardware are not stated [published] — [model card](https://huggingface.co/lowlevelware/512x512_diffusion_unconditional_ImageNet)
- MMagic lists the portrait generator's dataset as "Unknown" and still marks the KaliYuga models "Coming soon" [code] — [MMagic Disco README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/open-mmlab/mmagic@main/configs/disco_diffusion/README.md)
- Disco ships different cut schedules for the KaliYuga models: heavy overview cuts and grayscale early, high `cut_ic_pow` 10-12 [code] — [disco.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alembics/disco-diffusion/main/disco.py)

### Inferences
- The KaliYuga architecture cannot load OpenAI's weights (128 against 256 channels, different attention layout), so those models were trained from scratch on about 4k images. A model of about 114M parameters on 4k images has a far narrower prior than ImageNet. That is why they produce a strong house style, and also why they need gentler, overview-heavy cut schedules. For "maximum quality from the unconditional 256 model" they are a different product, not an upgrade.
- Memory for fine-tuning the real 553M model on 12 GB (RTX 4070), estimated: fp32 weights 2.2 GB, gradients 2.2 GB and Adam moments 4.4 GB come to about 8.8 GB before activations, which matches Sxela's report that it did not fit a Colab GPU at batch 1. It becomes feasible with an 8-bit optimizer or Adafactor, gradient checkpointing (`use_checkpoint=True` exists in guided-diffusion), fp16/bf16 autocast and batch 1-4 with gradient accumulation, or by training only part of the network (later blocks, attention, LoRA on attention projections). These are estimates, not runs.
- The only published "curated fine-tune" data point that bears on CLIP-guided quality is indirect. The 512 model's 8,100-step fine-tune exists because the class-conditional model guided worse without a class. A curated-aesthetic fine-tune of the 256 model, for example LAION-aesthetic photos above 6.5, a few thousand to tens of thousands of steps, should be judged by the protocol in question 5 against the stock checkpoint. Expected sign: better aesthetic rating, possibly lower prompt fit if the data are narrow.

### Gaps
- I found no training hyperparameters (learning rate, batch, steps, hardware, time) for any KaliYuga model, the portrait generator or the 512 unconditional fine-tune. KaliYuga's guide and the Patreon posts are not readable.
- I found no side-by-side comparison, metric or human, of a fine-tuned model against the stock 256/512 model under the same CLIP guidance.
- "Medieval diffusion" and "liminal diffusion" ADM fine-tunes were not found under those names. Searches returned only Stable Diffusion fine-tunes.

## 4. Cascades and refinement that fit in 8 GB and could raise a 256 unconditional output

### Takeaway
Three routes exist, and the evidence ranks them. (1) **Base-then-upsample** with OpenAI's class-conditional upsamplers. Here at 256, a 64 px base plus the upsampler lost 0.05-0.07 to direct 256 (class-conditional base), and there is no unconditional 64 or 128 base at all, so this route does not improve a 256 unconditional pipeline. Its strength is 128→512. (2) **SDEdit-style refinement with SD-Turbo.** Here it raised a 128 px ADM image's held-out score by +0.014 to +0.06 depending on strength, but always landed below plain SD-Turbo and kept ADM's semantic mistakes. It is a texture fixer, not a composition fixer. (3) **GAN upscalers** (Real-ESRGAN x4plus) are cheap and tie or help at 512, but cannot be seen by 224 px judges. SDEdit's own paper puts the faithfulness sweet spot at t0 0.3-0.6, with up to 3 repeats.

### Cited Findings

**ADM upsamplers and cascades**
- OpenAI ships `64_256_upsampler.pt` (1,247,310,648 bytes) and `128_512_upsampler.pt` (1,235,462,529 bytes). Both are **class-conditional** and condition on a bilinearly upsampled low-resolution image. There is no 256→512 upsampler, and the ImageNet 64 and 128 base models are also class-conditional [published] — [guided-diffusion README](https://github.com/openai/guided-diffusion); details in [refinement_pipelines.md §1](../CLIP%20guided%20diffusion%20quality%20improvements/refinement_pipelines.md)
- ADM FID at 256: ADM 10.94, upsampled ADM-U 7.49, guided ADM-G 4.59, ADM-G+ADM-U 3.94. "When combining classifier guidance with upsampling, we only guide the lower resolution model" [published] — [ADM paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05233)
- The ADM upsamplers were trained only on clean area-downsampled photographs, with no noise-conditioning augmentation, unlike CDM and Imagen, which call that augmentation crucial [code/published] — [super_res_train.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/guided-diffusion/main/scripts/super_res_train.py); [CDM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.15282)
- GLIDE: a noised CLIP guiding the ImageNet 64 model, followed by a diffusion upsampler, was "equal or higher quality" than the community 256/512 notebooks [published] — [GLIDE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10741)
- Here, against direct class-conditional 256 (12 prompts, seed 0, three held-out judges): 64 + upsampler unguided −0.068/−0.058/−0.043, guided −0.052/−0.045/−0.035, 64 + x4plus −0.065/−0.041/−0.035, 128 alone −0.020/+0.004/−0.010. At 512, 128 + upsampler was +0.010/+0.020/+0.014 and 128 + HAT +0.016/+0.021/+0.018. Guidance on the upsampler added +0.007 to +0.016 at 256 and +0.001 to +0.002 at 512. Peak VRAM per stage was at most 4.1 GiB [measured here] — [refinement_pipelines.md §1](../CLIP%20guided%20diffusion%20quality%20improvements/refinement_pipelines.md)
- The 128 + Real-ESRGAN route to 512 tied on seed 1, −0.0135 ± 0.028 (6 of 12), with aesthetic rating 4.83 against 5.32 and 10.4% clipped pixels against 4.1%, at about a sixth of the time. It shipped as `-up`, off by default [measured here] — [report](../../reports/CLIP%20guided%20diffusion%20quality%20improvements.md)
- Disco's own "superres" (v4.1 to v5.0) was LDSR 4x as a sharpening pass back to the original size, and it was removed in v5.1 [code] — [disco-diffusion README](https://github.com/alembics/disco-diffusion)

**SDEdit and img2img refinement**
- SDEdit: raising t0 increases realism (KID) and decreases faithfulness (L2 to the guide). "t0 ∈ [0.3, 0.6] works well" for reasonable guides, with binary search on one image for fixed tasks. Guides far from realistic need larger t0, at a cost in faithfulness. Repeating perturb-and-denoise K = 1-3 times "work[s] reasonably well", and larger K is more realistic. Humans preferred SDEdit over GAN baselines by up to 98.09% on realism and 91.72% on satisfaction on LSUN bedroom strokes [published] — [SDEdit](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.01073)
- SD-Turbo is a distilled SD 2.1 at a fixed 512x512 that "does not achieve perfect photorealism". For Turbo img2img, `num_inference_steps * strength` must be ≥ 1. Diffusers warns to expect quality loss at non-native sizes [published] — [SD-Turbo card](https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/sd-turbo/raw/main/README.md); [Diffusers SDXL Turbo docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/using-diffusers/sdxl_turbo)
- Here: SD-Turbo img2img over 128 px class-conditional ADM images (bicubic to 512, VAE encode, noise to `t0 = strength*999`, 1 step, prompt-conditioned), 12 prompts, seed 0, mean scores (ViT-B/16, ViT-H, SigLIP2, aesthetic). Base ADM: .289 / .277 / .087 / 4.72. Strength .3: .303 / .297 / .120 / 4.76. Strength .5: .303 / .306 / .126 / 4.93. Strength .7: .322 / .332 / .147 / 5.37. Strength .7 with 2 steps: .324 / .331 / .143 / 5.33. Plain SD-Turbo from noise, 1 step: .340 / .373 / .184 / 6.31. SD-Turbo + ReNO (B/32+PE): .359 / .386 / .196 / 6.47 [reanalysis] — [judge_scores.json](../../reports/evidence/scores/judge_scores.json); script `refine()` in [exp.py](../../reports/evidence/scripts/exp.py)
- The report's summary of the same run: "Img2img +.023 to +.049 by strength, and it inherits ADM's errors: the pencil sketch stays a pen" [measured here] — [report](../../reports/CLIP%20guided%20diffusion%20quality%20improvements.md)
- VRAM here on the RTX 2070 SUPER: SD-Turbo at 512 takes 2.5 GiB plain and 3.2 GiB with CLIP. SDXL-Lightning at 1024 takes 7.3 GiB plain [measured here] — [README, Modern backbones](../../README.md)

### Inferences
- **For a 256 unconditional output specifically:** no OpenAI unconditional 64 or 128 base exists, so "compose at 64, upsample to 256" means switching to the class-conditional 64 model, where the 64→256 cascade already lost 0.05 here, or to GLIDE's text-conditional 64→256 upsampler (1.59 GB, untested here). Neither is a refinement of the unconditional model's own output.
- The workable cascade for the unconditional model is **256 unconditional → SDEdit at 512 with SD-Turbo (strength 0.3-0.5, optionally with CLIP noise optimisation on the injected noise) → downsample or keep 512**. It fits easily in 8 GB, since ADM and Turbo run sequentially. The numbers above predict a gain in held-out CLIP and aesthetic score over the ADM image, but a result that stays below plain SD-Turbo. The 0.7 point also overwrites most of ADM's content, so the "ADM image" survives only as a layout prior. Whether that is worth it depends on whether ADM's layouts are ever better than SD-Turbo's own. That is testable: score per prompt and count the prompts where refine-at-.5 beats plain Turbo.
- Composition preservation: strength ≤ 0.5 keeps layout and colour (SDEdit's faithfulness side), and 0.6-0.7 rewrites texture and fine objects. Bicubic resizing to 512 before encoding mattered more than the upscaler choice in my reading, because SD-Turbo's VAE sees 512.
- An ADM-native alternative that stays within the unconditional model is SDEdit with the unconditional model itself: `-init` its own output and `-skip` 0.3-0.5 with stronger guidance, which is Disco's "resume from partial" practice. It costs a second ADM pass and has no evidence here.
- Real-ESRGAN or HAT after the final 256/512 image is free (≤ 0.7 GiB) but invisible to 224 px judges. Gains there need a native-resolution judge (crops at 224 of a 512 image) or human eyes.

### Gaps
- No refinement of an *unconditional* 256 output was run here. All SDEdit numbers are from 128 px class-conditional bases on one seed, and the ADM upsampler numbers use class-conditional bases.
- GLIDE's upsampler, SDXL-Turbo img2img and ControlNet-tile refinement were not run. I found no published measurement of composition preservation (for example LPIPS or DINO similarity to the input) as a function of Turbo img2img strength.
- No published result runs CLIP guidance on an SDEdit refinement pass of a CLIP-guided pixel-diffusion image.

## 5. How to evaluate gains reliably without being fooled by the guide

### Takeaway
Use judges that did not guide (already this repo's practice), but add a judge from a different family: VQAScore for prompt fit, HPSv3 or ImageReward for preference, and a native-resolution check for detail. Never select or tune on the judge that reports. On this repo's own saved scores, the paired per-image difference between two settings has a seed-level SD of about 0.029 and a prompt-level effect SD of 0.011 (ViT-B/16) to 0.018 (pooled). Detecting a 0.01 difference at 80% power then needs about 80-90 prompts × 1 seed, or 27-41 prompts × 4 seeds. A 0.005 difference needs 310-350 prompts × 1 seed, or 110-160 × 4. The current 12 prompts × 4 seeds resolve only about 0.015-0.02. Published work shows CLIPScore rankings flip across seeds even at 1,000 prompts, and that single rewards get hacked while ensembles of rewards only partly resist.

### Cited Findings

**Judges and how well they agree with people**
- VQAScore (CLIP-FlanT5) agreed with human ratings best on GenAI-Bench (about 1,600 compositional prompts), ahead of CLIPScore, PickScore and Davidsonian. Baseline pairwise accuracy on GenAI-Bench images: PickScore 57.1, ImageReward 56.6, CLIPScore 50.8, HPSv2 49.6. In best-of-N ranking, VQAScore improved human ratings 2-3x more than PickScore, HPSv2 or ImageReward [published] — [GenAI-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.13743); [VQAScore](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.01291)
- HPSv3 reports pairwise accuracy of 76.9% on HPDv3, 72.8% on the Pick-a-Pic test set and 85.4% on HPDv2, beating HPSv2 by 19.3 points and PickScore by 17.2 on HPDv3. License CC BY-NC-SA [published] — [HPSv3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03789)
- PickScore claims "superhuman performance" at predicting human preferences, recommends itself for evaluation, and recommends Pick-a-Pic prompts over MS-COCO [published] — [Pick-a-Pic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01569)
- ImageReward was trained on 137k expert comparisons and is offered as "a promising automatic metric" [published] — [ImageReward](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05977)
- GenEval checks objects, counts, positions, colours and co-occurrence with an object detector, and agrees with humans "significantly" better than CLIPScore on compositional tasks [published] — [GenEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11513)
- CMMD (CLIP embeddings + MMD with an RBF kernel) is unbiased and depends far less on sample size than FID, which assumes normal Inception features and is inconsistent across sample sizes [published] — [Rethinking FID, CVPR 2024](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Jayasumana_Rethinking_FID_Towards_a_Better_Evaluation_Metric_for_Image_Generation_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf); [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/rethinking-fid-towards-a-better-evaluation-metric-for-image-generation/)
- GLIDE found that humans disagree with CLIP score [published] — [GLIDE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10741)

**Prompt sets** (sizes as reported by ReNO)
- T2I-CompBench 6,000 compositional prompts, GenEval 552 object-focused prompts, Parti-Prompts more than 1,600 [published] — [ReNO](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04312v2)

**Seed robustness and significance**
- On 1,000 COCO prompts with 3 seeds, CLIPScore's model ranking flipped between seeds (PixArt 25.75 against SD1.5 25.76 at seed 5096), and so did DSGScore's. VQAScore's ranking stayed stable. A +1 pixel-value perturbation moved CLIPScore by 0.74 on average and 7.30 at worst. Paired t-tests at p < 0.05: a VQAScore gap of 1.15 points was "significant", yet the "worse" model won 50% of prompts against 49%. SD3 beat SDXL on only 60% of prompts despite a 4.5-point gap. Their conclusion is to report statistical significance and per-prompt dominance together [published] — [Re-Thinking the Automatic Evaluation of Image-Text Alignment](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08480)
- Paired differences of metric scores were non-normal in another T2I study, which switched to Wilcoxon signed-rank tests [published] — [arXiv 2401.16092](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.16092)

**Judge hacking**
- ReNO on SD-Turbo: optimising ImageReward alone lowered the held-out LAION aesthetic score from 5.647 to 5.611, and CLIP alone lowered it to 5.475. The combination of HPSv2, PickScore, ImageReward and CLIP (weights 5.0, 0.05, 1.0, 1.0) raised it to 5.704. A leave-one-out on Parti-Prompts showed that the omitted reward still improved on 80.4% (CLIPScore) to 94.7% (HPSv2) of images. The AMT study used just over 1,000 prompts with an average of 495 raters per comparison, and the authors warn that AMT "can potentially be noisy" [published] — [ReNO](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04312v2)
- Ensembling rewards only partly mitigates hacking in T2I RL. The typical failure is images with unrealistic artifacts that still score high, and the paper adds an artifact reward as a regulariser [published] — [Understanding Reward Hacking in T2I RL](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03468)
- Pointwise HPSv2 rewards drift toward oversaturation, and PickScore optimisation can trade away image quality [published, via overviews] — [Adv-GRPO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.20256); [Pref-GRPO overview](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2508.20751v2)
- Using ReNO to probe reward models, PickScore produced the largest increase in NSFW classifications, and HPS and ImageReward pushed diverse subjects toward White subjects [published] — [Bias at the End of the Score](https://vcg.seas.harvard.edu/publications/bias-at-the-end-of-the-score/paper)

**This repo's current protocol and its variance**
- Judges ViT-B/16, ViT-H-14-quickgelu and ViT-L-16-SigLIP2-256 never guide, and scores are paired on shared sampler noise with a 95% CI across prompts. Stated limits: judges resize to about 224 px and cannot see fine detail; no human study; 27 to 55 uncorrected arms per design, so 2-4 false "wins" are expected by chance; effects under about 0.01 are unresolved [measured here] — [report, How to read the evidence](../../reports/CLIP%20guided%20diffusion%20quality%20improvements.md)
- A one-way random-effects decomposition of 28 four-seed arms against base (ADM 128 class-conditional, 12 prompts × seeds 1-4, paired on seed). Per-image paired-difference SD, median over arms: ViT-B/16 0.029 (seed component) with a prompt-effect SD of 0.011. Pooled over 3 judges: 0.028 and 0.018. For the base arm, the within-prompt seed SD of raw scores is 0.019 (B/16) and the between-prompt SD of means is 0.036 [reanalysis] — [judge_scores.json](../../reports/evidence/scores/judge_scores.json)

### Inferences
- **Protocol for the unconditional 256 work:**
  1. Freeze a held-out prompt set of at least 100 prompts, never used for tuning: for example 50 PartiPrompts across categories, 25 GenEval-style compositional prompts and 25 of the repo's own. Tune on a separate set of 12-24.
  2. One seed per prompt for screening, which detects about 0.01. Four seeds on 40+ prompts for confirmation. Pair arms on prompt and seed (same initial noise).
  3. Report the paired mean difference with a bootstrap or Wilcoxon interval, the per-prompt win rate (dominance), and the number of arms tested. Adjust with Holm or accept a stated false-discovery rate.
  4. Judges: keep the three CLIP judges, and add VQAScore (prompt fit, different family, most seed-stable), HPSv3 or ImageReward (preference) and an aesthetic predictor. Never let a judge that reports also select (`-best`) or guide. If `-best` uses ViT-H, report on the other judges only.
  5. Detail: score 224 px crops of 512 outputs, or use a 448/384 px judge (SigLIP2-384, PE-Core-L-336), because 224 px judges cannot see what upscalers or refiners change.
  6. Distribution: CMMD against a few hundred LAION-aesthetic or COCO images to catch a collapse in style or diversity. It is cheap and stable at small n, where FID is not.
  7. Humans: blind pairwise A/B on the final two candidates. Detecting a 60/40 preference at 80% power needs about 194 pairs, and 65/35 needs about 85 (binomial power arithmetic). One person rating 100 pairs in a sitting is feasible.
- At about 20 s per unconditional 256 image on the 2070 SUPER, a 100-prompt, 1-seed screening arm costs about 35 minutes and a 40 × 4 confirmation about 55 minutes. The design is affordable.
- Hacking checks specific to CLIP guidance: a gain that appears on ViT-B/32 or PE (the guides) but not on ViT-H, SigLIP2 or VQAScore is the guide being fooled. A gain on CLIP judges with a falling aesthetic or HPS score, or rising saturation or clipped-pixel percentage, is adversarial texture. The repo already logs saturation and clip%, and those should be required columns.
- The variance figures come from the class-conditional 128 pipeline. The unconditional 256 model has no class anchor, so its seed variance is probably larger. The first unconditional run should re-estimate σ_seed and σ_prompt before the sample sizes above are trusted.

### Gaps
- No published sample-size guidance exists for CLIP-score differences of 0.005-0.01. The table above is this repo's own variance, from a different model and size.
- I could not read PickScore's, HPSv2's or ImageReward's own accuracy tables (abstracts only), nor DrawBench's prompt count from a primary source.
- No study measures how well VQAScore, HPSv3 or ImageReward rank old, adversarially textured CLIP-guided ADM images. All of them were validated on modern model outputs, which these images do not resemble.
