## 1. Language-free / image-embedding conditioning: how LAFITE, unCLIP, KNN-Diffusion, CLOOB latent diffusion and cc12m_1 train, and how they bridge text and image embeddings

### Takeaway
An image-embedding-conditioned decoder can be trained from unlabelled images, but at sampling the CLIP text embedding is not where the image embeddings were, so every method bridges the gap: LAFITE perturbs the image embedding with normalised Gaussian noise during training (zero-shot COCO FID 26.94), KNN-Diffusion conditions on retrieved image neighbours (COCO FID 12.5 vs 32.8 without them), and unCLIP learns a prior (FID 7.99 vs 16.55 when the text embedding is fed zero-shot to an image-embedding decoder). The cheapest bridges that a single person can build are LAFITE-style noise, kNN retrieval over a local CLIP index, or avoiding the gap by training on text embeddings of captions (cc12m_1 was "conditioned on CLIP text embeddings").

### Cited Findings
- LAFITE (Zhou et al., CVPR 2022) is "the first method to train text-to-image models without any text data", replacing text conditioning with features generated from CLIP image features. — [LAFITE abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13792)
- LAFITE's fixed perturbation adds Gaussian noise scaled to the image feature's norm (level ξ) and re-normalises; a trainable variant uses two 4-layer MLPs to shift the feature and set per-dimension noise, trained to match real text features (needs some paired data). The paper reports no ablation of ξ and does not state the ξ values used. — [LAFITE full text (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.13792)
- LAFITE results on MS-COCO: language-free LafiteG IS 27.20 / FID 18.04, LafiteNN IS 22.23 / FID 26.56, captioning baseline FID 47.21; fully supervised FID 8.12; zero-shot after CC3M pretraining FID 26.94 vs DALL-E 27.50 and CogView 27.10 (CogView better on blurred FID-1/FID-2). 75M trainable parameters, 4 V100s, about 4 days from scratch on COCO to FID 18, fine-tuning about 3 hours. CLIP ViT-B/16 conditioning gave FID 8.12 vs ViT-B/32 8.62 (supervised). — [LAFITE (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.13792)
- unCLIP's decoder (GLIDE architecture, 3.5B parameters) projects the CLIP image embedding and adds it to the timestep embedding, and also projects it into four extra context tokens concatenated to the text-encoder outputs; the original text pathway "offers little benefit". Trained 800K iterations at batch 2048, lr 1.2e-4, on ~250M images. — [unCLIP (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.06125)
- unCLIP Section 5.1: conditioning on the caption alone is the worst option; feeding CLIP text embeddings zero-shot to the image-embedding decoder "produces reasonable results"; FIDs: small decoder trained on text embeddings 9.16, small unCLIP stack with diffusion prior 7.99, text embeddings fed zero-shot to the unCLIP decoder 16.55. Humans preferred the full stack over the text-embedding decoder 57.0% ± 3.1% (photorealism) and 53.1% ± 3.1% (caption similarity). — [unCLIP (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.06125)
- MS-COCO zero-shot FID (unCLIP Table 2): DALL-E ~28, LAFITE 26.94, GLIDE 12.24, Make-A-Scene 11.84, unCLIP AR prior 10.63, unCLIP diffusion prior 10.39. — [unCLIP (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.06125)
- KNN-Diffusion trains on images only (PMD, 69M pairs with text discarded; stickers 400M image-only), conditioning the decoder on the training image's CLIP image embedding plus its k nearest neighbours from a non-trainable FAISS index (IVF + OPQ, 2048→256 dims to fit a 128 GB RAM server); at inference the CLIP text embedding queries the index. k=10 was best. — [KNN-Diffusion (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.02849)
- KNN-Diffusion zero-shot MS-COCO FID 12.5 vs LAFITE 26.9, FuseDream 21.2 and its own no-kNN ablation 32.8; humans preferred it to LAFITE 72.1% (quality) and 65.3% (text alignment). Shrinking the index to 10% of PMD only moved FID from 13.65 to 13.92. It describes the text–image gap as CLIP embeddings alone "cannot accurately bridge". No CLIP score or training compute reported. — [KNN-Diffusion (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.02849)
- CLOOB-conditioned latent diffusion (JD-P / Crowson lineage) trains on CLOOB image embeddings and samples with CLOOB text embeddings, because CLOOB text and image embeds "share a high similarity of somewhere around 0.9", which "makes it possible to train the model without captions". The 1.2B yfcc_cfg model took about 5.5 days on 8x A100 to 250k steps; no quantitative quality results are given. — [cloob-latent-diffusion README](https://github.com/JD-P/cloob-latent-diffusion)
- Crowson's cc12m_1_cfg: "A 602M parameter CLIP conditioned model trained on Conceptual 12M for 3.1M steps", then "fine-tuned for classifier-free guidance for 250K additional steps", 256x256; cfg_sample.py defaults to 50 PLMS steps and prompt weight 3. The README does not name the CLIP model or the dropout rate, and reports no FID. — [v-diffusion-pytorch README](https://github.com/crowsonkb/v-diffusion-pytorch)
- Crowson's Replicate page describes the model as "a diffusion model conditioned on CLIP text embeddings" using classifier-free guidance, with the v objective and continuous timesteps. — [Replicate clip-guided-diffusion-cfg](https://replicate.com/crowsonkb/clip-guided-diffusion-cfg/readme)

### Inferences
- The modality gap is the central risk of language-free conditioning with OpenAI CLIP: CLOOB's ~0.9 text–image similarity is cited as what made caption-free training work, while unCLIP's zero-shot text-embedding decoder lost about 2x in FID (16.55 vs 7.99). With OpenAI CLIP, plan for one of: LAFITE noise (cheap, no data), kNN retrieval (needs a CLIP index of the training set, which this repo can build with its own `clip_models`), or a learned text→image mapping/prior (needs pairs).
- The simplest way to avoid the gap entirely is to condition on what will be given at sampling: CLIP text embeddings of captions (cc12m_1's approach per Replicate). Captioned sources exist (CC12M alt-text, PD12M synthetic captions, prompts of synthetic images). Training on a mix of image embeddings (with LAFITE noise) and text embeddings is a natural hybrid; it is not tested in any source found.
- For this repo specifically, image prompts (`-ip`-style use) become free: an image-embedding-conditioned model accepts an image embedding directly with no gap.
- Expected gain is not measured by any source for an ADM-scale pixel model at this compute. The literature's ordering (CFG-conditioned models beat CLIP-guided unconditional ones in GLIDE's human study; KNN/LAFITE reach zero-shot COCO FID 12.5–27 at 75–400M parameters) suggests a conditioned 553M model is a stronger prior than the unconditional one, but whether a 1–2 week single-GPU adaptation reaches the 0.35 CLIP similarity of SD-Turbo + ReNO is unknown and must be measured with the repo's held-out benchmark. (Estimate.)

### Gaps
- The CLIP variant and condition-dropout rate of cc12m_1 are not stated in any fetched source (the task brief's "ViT-B/16 image embeddings" could not be confirmed; Replicate says text embeddings).
- No CLIP-score numbers for LAFITE/KNN-Diffusion/CLOOB-LD; LAFITE's ξ values unreported.
- No source quantifies the gain of conditioning a pretrained ADM checkpoint specifically.

## 2. How to inject a CLIP embedding into ADM, and which way is cheapest to train from the unconditional checkpoint without destroying it

### Takeaway
The cheapest, exactly-non-destructive injection is a zero-initialised (bias-free) projection of the CLIP embedding added to the 1024-d time embedding, which is precisely how the class-conditional ADM checkpoints carry their class (`emb = emb + label_emb(y)`) and how unCLIP feeds its decoder; it reaches every ResBlock through AdaGN scale/shift. Extra tokens (unCLIP's 4 context tokens, GLIDE cross-attention) need new attention inputs because this U-Net's attention blocks have none; ControlNet-style side networks are designed for spatial conditions and cost ~23% more memory and ~34% more time per step in SD.

### Cited Findings
- ADM conditions through AdaGN: "AdaGN(h,y) = y_s GroupNorm(h) + y_b", with scale and shift from "a linear projection of the timestep and class embedding"; replacing AdaGN with addition + GroupNorm worsened FID 13.06 → 15.08. — [ADM (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.05233)
- In the installed guided-diffusion, the class embedding is a `nn.Embedding(num_classes, time_embed_dim)` added to the time embedding: `emb = emb + self.label_emb(y)` (unet.py lines 511, 691). — local file `/home/sam/GitHub/clip-guided-diffusion/.venv/lib/python3.13/site-packages/guided_diffusion/unet.py` (crowsonkb's guided-diffusion fork, [GitHub](https://github.com/crowsonkb/guided-diffusion))
- GLIDE conditions ADM on text by using the final token embedding "in place of a class embedding" and projecting the full token sequence to each attention layer's dimensionality, concatenated to that layer's attention context. — [GLIDE (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.10741)
- unCLIP adds the projected CLIP image embedding to the timestep embedding and projects it into four extra context tokens. — [unCLIP (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.06125)
- ControlNet: a trainable copy of the encoder joined by zero convolutions ("a 1×1 convolution layer with both weight and bias initialized to zeros"), so at the first step the output equals the original and "harmful noise cannot influence the hidden states"; it needs "about 23% more GPU memory and 34% more time in each training iteration" than plain SD fine-tuning; models learn the condition abruptly ("sudden convergence"), "usually in less than 10K optimization steps". — [ControlNet (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2302.05543)
- A 2024 paper adds LoRA adapters to the attention layers of a U-Net diffusion model as a conditioning mechanism and reports better FID on EDM, noting standard U-Nets apply scale-and-shift conditioning only to conv blocks (seen only through a search summary, not read in full). — [arXiv 2405.03958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03958)

### Inferences
- Concrete design A (recommended first): `cond_proj = MLP(D_clip → 1024 → 1024)` whose last Linear is zero-initialised and bias-free, output added to `emb` right where `label_emb` would be (unet.py:691). At step 0 the model is bit-identical to the unconditional checkpoint. Null condition for CFG = zero vector, which maps to exactly zero. Trainable set, cheapest to richest: (a) `cond_proj` only (~1–2M parameters; conditioning then must work through the frozen `emb_layers`); (b) plus the 42 `emb_layers` (53.0M, 9.6%), which are the AdaGN path the class-conditional checkpoint uses to steer content; (c) plus LoRA on `qkv`/`proj_out` (51.4M base) and the 3x3 convs (447M base). Training (b) or (c) also changes the unconditional branch, because the time embedding passes through the same layers.
- Design B (more expressive, still exact at init): add a decoupled cross-attention (IP-Adapter-like; inference, not from a fetched source) to each of the 16 AttentionBlocks: queries from the block's normalised input, keys/values from N condition tokens (unCLIP used 4; CLIP patch tokens or several CLIP models' embeddings are options), output through a zero-initialised projection added to the residual. Appending tokens to the existing self-attention (GLIDE-style) instead is not identity at init even with zero K/V, because the extra logits (q·0 = 0) dilute the softmax. Rough size at 3 new projections of C×C per block: ~11×3×1024² + 5×3×512² ≈ 38M parameters (estimate).
- Alternative initialisation: the class-conditional `256x256_diffusion.pt` (already cached in `~/.cache/clip-guided-diffusion/`) has a trained 1000×1024 `label_emb`. A CLIP→`label_emb` regressor pre-trained on ImageNet (target: the image's class embedding) would give a sensible conditioning signal on step 0 instead of zero, and the repo already maps prompts to the closest class. Not tested anywhere found; it is a cheap experiment.
- ControlNet's trainable encoder copy (~half the U-Net) is overkill for a single global vector; its zero-init idea is what matters and design A already has it.
- The CLIP model used for conditioning should not be the one used as the judge (the repo's held-out judges), see Q6.

### Gaps
- No source compares time-embedding injection vs cross-attention tokens for a pretrained pixel ADM at small compute; unCLIP used both but at 3.5B/800K×2048 scale.
- IP-Adapter's decoupled cross-attention numbers were not fetched in this pass.

## 3. Classifier-free guidance via condition dropout, guidance scales, and combining CFG with CLIP gradient guidance

### Takeaway
Published dropout rates are 10–20% for the condition (GLIDE 20% empty captions, unCLIP 10% zeroed CLIP embeddings plus 50% dropped captions, ControlNet 50% empty prompts); guidance scales ranged 1.25 (unCLIP decoder) to 3 (GLIDE human eval, cc12m_1_cfg default weight 3). In GLIDE's human study CFG beat CLIP guidance decisively (Elo 82.7 vs −73.2 photorealism, 110.9 vs 29.3 caption), and CLIP guidance was suspected of producing adversarial examples for the scoring CLIP; Crowson shipped both CFG and CLIP-guided sampling for her CLIP-conditioned models, so the two compose.

### Cited Findings
- GLIDE fine-tuned for CFG by replacing "20% of text token sequences ... with the empty sequence". Human Elo with CFG scale 3.0 and CLIP scale 2.0: unguided −88.6 / −106.2, CLIP guidance −73.2 / 29.3, CFG 82.7 / 110.9 (photorealism / caption). CFG is "(nearly) Pareto optimal" on precision/recall and IS/FID, while CLIP guidance raises CLIP score far more; GLIDE hypothesises "CLIP guidance is finding adversarial examples for the evaluation CLIP model". Best CFG zero-shot COCO FID 12.24 at scale 1.5. GLIDE-filtered (300M) was pre-trained 1.1M iterations then fine-tuned 500K for CFG and inpainting. — [GLIDE (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.10741)
- unCLIP: CLIP embeddings set to zero (or a learned embedding) 10% of the time, caption dropped 50%; decoder guidance scale 1.25 for the COCO comparison; guiding the prior hurt; GLIDE at scale 2.0 was close in photorealism and caption similarity but less diverse. — [unCLIP (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.06125)
- ControlNet randomly replaces 50% of text prompts with empty strings during training. — [ControlNet (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2302.05543)
- afiaka87/glide-finetune uses `uncond_p` 0.2 for base and latent training, 0.0 for the upsampler, lr 1e-4 in pixel space, bf16. — [glide-finetune README](https://github.com/afiaka87/glide-finetune)
- cc12m_1_cfg was obtained by a separate 250K-step CFG fine-tune after 3.1M conditional steps; cfg_sample.py: 50 PLMS steps, weight 3; clip_sample.py uses cc12m_1 (CLIP-conditioned) with CLIP guidance, 1000 steps, `--clip-guidance-scale` default 500. — [v-diffusion-pytorch README](https://github.com/crowsonkb/v-diffusion-pytorch)
- Crowson's CFG model page states that guided-diffusion sampling scripts, "specifically CLIP guided diffusion", are included with the CFG CLIP-text-conditioned model. — [Replicate clip-guided-diffusion-cfg](https://replicate.com/crowsonkb/clip-guided-diffusion-cfg/readme)

### Inferences
- For this repo: train with p_uncond ≈ 0.1–0.2 (zero vector as null), sample with CFG scale ~1.5–4 swept on the held-out prompts, and keep the existing capped CLIP gradient guidance on top at a reduced rate. CFG doubles U-Net evaluations per step (cond + uncond, batchable as batch 2 in fp16 on the 8 GB 2070S); with learned sigma, guide the ε channels and keep the conditional variance channels (the GLIDE sampling convention; recalled from glide-text2im, not fetched here).
- A variant that keeps the unconditional branch exactly the pretrained model: with LoRA/adapters, run the unconditional pass with adapters disabled. Then CFG pushes away from the original ImageNet prior as well as toward the condition. No dropout is then needed for the null branch, but it conflates domain shift with conditioning; worth an A/B. (Untested inference.)
- Because the CLIP-guidance term can be adversarial to its own CLIP family, combining CFG (non-adversarial, from the model) with modest CLIP guidance (already capped in `sampler.py`) is likely the best mix, but the scales must be retuned since the conditioned model already moves toward the prompt.

### Gaps
- Ho & Salimans' "Classifier-Free Diffusion Guidance" (arXiv 2207.12598) dropout/scale ablations were not fetched in this pass.
- No source gives a measured CFG+CLIP-guidance combination on cc12m_1 (scales, scores).

## 4. Parameter-efficient vs full training of the 553M pixel U-Net on 12 GB: memory, throughput, GPU-days

### Takeaway
Both fit on 12 GB at 256x256 with activation checkpointing: frozen-trunk adapters (measured 3.18 GiB at batch 1, 4.28 GiB at batch 2 on fp32 weights with fp16 autocast, ~1.1 GiB per extra sample) and a full fine-tune with 8-bit AdamW (~5.5 GB of weights, grads and optimizer state before EMA and activations). Full fp32 AdamW plus an on-GPU EMA (~11 GB before activations) does not fit. Expect roughly 2–3 images/s on an RTX 4070 (estimate from ADM's 6.4 images/V100-s), i.e. ~170k–260k training samples per day: a ~0.4–2M-sample adaptation is 2–10 GPU-days, about 0.1–0.4% of the model's ImageNet pretraining.

### Cited Findings
- ADM training throughput at 256x256 (Table 7): theoretical 17.9 images per V100-second; naive per-GPU batch 4 reaches 4.4 (25% utilisation); optimised batch 8 reaches 6.4 (36%) with fused GroupNorm-Swish and fused Adam. — [ADM (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.05233)
- ADM 256 hyperparameters: 554M parameters, batch 256, lr 1e-4, 1980K iterations (conditional); unconditional and conditional ImageNet 256 models "were trained for 2M iterations ... with batch size 256"; 16-bit training with loss scaling but 32-bit weights, EMA (0.9999) and optimizer state. ADM-G 256 (1980K) cost 916 V100-days for the generator. — [ADM (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.05233)
- ControlNet: SD depth ControlNet trained with "200k training samples, one single NVIDIA RTX 3090Ti, and 5 days of training", competitive with SDv2-D2I trained on "thousands of GPU hours". — [ControlNet (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2302.05543)
- CLOOB latent diffusion: "finetunes tend to take 10-20% of the resources that the original training run did". — [cloob-latent-diffusion README](https://github.com/JD-P/cloob-latent-diffusion)
- A community WikiArt fine-tune of CLOOB latent diffusion ran ~12 hours on 2x A6000 and visibly shifted style toward paintings. — [Whitaker, W&B report](https://wandb.ai/johnowhitaker/jw-ft-cloob-latent-diffusion/reports/Fine-Tuning-CLOOB-latent-diffusion--VmlldzoxNzk5OTgz)
- glide-finetune (pixel GLIDE) trains in bf16 at batch 4–8 with `--activation_checkpointing`, and warns that `torch.compile` on pixel-space bf16 models causes "training outputs to slowly degrade into abstract blobs over time", attributed to incorrect gradients through mixed-precision ResBlocks. — [glide-finetune README](https://github.com/afiaka87/glide-finetune)
- LoRA on pixel-space DDPM U-Nets has been rank-swept on CIFAR-10 (ranks 2–32, FID/params/memory) in a 2026 study; no source applies LoRA/LoCon to the ADM/guided-diffusion convs (search summaries only). — [arXiv 2609.10656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10656)
- Local measurement (this session, RTX 2070 SUPER, PyTorch 2.9.1, random weights, fp32 weights + fp16 autocast, non-reentrant `torch.utils.checkpoint` per U-Net block, trunk frozen, zero-init 512→1024 projection added to the time embedding trainable, gradient flowing through every block): batch 1: 1012 ms/step, 0.99 img/s, peak 3.18 GiB; batch 2: 1805 ms/step, 1.11 img/s, peak 4.28 GiB. Caveat: another process was using the same GPU at 100% utilisation and 2.5–3.7 GiB during the run, so these speeds understate an idle GPU (likely by up to ~2x), and every larger configuration and every full-gradient configuration ran out of memory for that reason, not for lack of 8 GB. The GPU then dropped off the PCIe bus (kernel `Xid 79, GPU has fallen off the bus` at 17:43:04, while the other process was running and this benchmark was idle), so no clean rerun was possible. Script: `/tmp/claude-1000/-home-sam-GitHub-clip-guided-diffusion/34d680cf-d9a7-412c-9f94-d042cb1cd642/scratchpad/bench_adm_train.py`.

### Inferences
- Memory budget for 552.8M parameters (arithmetic; estimates): fp32 weights 2.21 GB, bf16 1.11 GB. (1) Adapters/LoRA, frozen trunk: measured ~2.1 GiB fixed + ~1.1 GiB/sample with block checkpointing → batch ~8 fits in 12 GB (storing the frozen trunk in bf16 saves ~1 GiB more). (2) Full fine-tune, fp32 AdamW: 2.21 weights + 2.21 grads + 4.42 Adam moments = 8.84 GB, + 2.21 GB EMA = 11.05 GB before activations → does not fit; keep EMA on CPU or skip it and it is still borderline at batch 1–2. (3) Full fine-tune, 8-bit AdamW (bitsandbytes stores the two moments in ~1 byte each; Dettmers et al., not fetched): 2.21 + 2.21 + 1.11 = 5.53 GB, + EMA 2.21 = 7.74 GB, + activations at batch 2 (~2.2 GiB) + autocast weight cache (~1.1 GiB) ≈ 11 GB → fits at batch 2 with gradient accumulation; EMA on CPU makes it comfortable.
- Throughput estimate for the RTX 4070: ADM's 36%-utilisation figure implies ~7 TFLOP per 256x256 training sample (assuming ADM's "theoretical" 17.9 img/s is against the V100's 125 TFLOPS fp16 tensor peak; the paper's basis was not checked). An RTX 4070's dense bf16 tensor rate with fp32 accumulation is ~58 TFLOPS (NVIDIA spec as I recall it; not fetched), and its memory bandwidth (~504 GB/s) is about half a V100's, which matters for GroupNorm-heavy ADM. At the same 25–36% utilisation that gives ~2–3 img/s for a full step without checkpointing and ~1.6–2.3 img/s with it; a frozen-trunk step with checkpointing costs about the same as a full step without (forward + recompute + activation-only backward), so ~2–3 img/s. The contended 2070 SUPER measurement (~1 img/s, half a GPU) is consistent with ~1.9 img/s predicted for an idle 2070 SUPER by the same model (taking its fp16-with-fp32-accumulate tensor peak as ~36 TFLOPS, from memory). All estimates; benchmark on the 4070 before committing.
- GPU-day budgets at ~2.5 img/s (estimate): 216k samples/day. ControlNet-style "sudden convergence" (<10K steps) at batch 8 = 80k samples ≈ 0.4 day. A cc12m_1_cfg-sized CFG phase (250K steps) at batch 8 = 2M samples ≈ 9 days. One ImageNet epoch (1.28M) ≈ 6 days. The pretraining (2M × 256 = 512M samples) is ~2,400 days at this rate, so a "10–20% of original" fine-tune (CLOOB-LD rule) is far out of reach; only a light adaptation (≲1% of pretraining) is feasible. Training cc12m_1-like conditioning from scratch (3.1M steps) is out of scope.
- Recommendation (inference): start with design A trainable set (b) — `cond_proj` + `emb_layers` (≈55M trainable) — under 8-bit AdamW or plain AdamW (only 55M parameters' states: ~0.9 GB), lr ~1e-4 for new layers and ~1e-5 for `emb_layers`, EMA ~0.999–0.9995 (the 0.9999 of ADM pretraining averages over ~10k steps, too long for a short run), bf16 autocast on the 4070 (fp32 master weights; no `torch.compile` per the glide-finetune warning), batch 8 by accumulation. Add LoRA (rank 16–64) on attention and convs, or move to a full 8-bit-Adam fine-tune, only if (b) plateaus. Measure CLIP similarity on held-out prompts every few thousand steps.
- Inference on the 8 GB 2070 SUPER is unchanged in cost except CFG's second U-Net pass. Weights trained in bf16/fp32 on Ada load into the repo's fp16 path (`convert_to_fp16`) the same way the original checkpoint does; the original was itself trained in fp16 with loss scaling, so fp16 range is unlikely to be a problem, but adapter outputs should be checked for overflow.
- Multi-day runs: checkpoint often (optimizer + EMA) given the GPU crash observed today on the 2070 SUPER machine; Xid 79 is usually power/PCIe/thermal, so consider a power limit for long runs. (Inference.)

## 5. Data: suitable open datasets, how much is needed, domain effects, licensing

### Takeaway
Language-free conditioning lets any image collection serve (ImageNet for minimal forgetting, plus broader aesthetic sets), while text-embedding conditioning needs captions: CC12M (~12M URL–alt-text pairs), PD12M (12.4M public-domain/CC0 images with synthetic captions, the cleanest licence), Re-LAION-5B (Apache-2.0 metadata, research-safe variant), DataComp (CC-BY-4.0 metadata, images keep their copyrights), JourneyDB (4.4M Midjourney images, custom gated terms), or prompts paired with synthetic images from the repo's own SD-Turbo/backbone pipeline. Precedents range from ~4k images (style fine-tune) to 200k (ControlNet competitive) to 4.8M (danbooru ADM); 100k–1M images is a reasonable target for conditioning (estimate).

### Cited Findings
- CC12M: "~12 million image-text pairs", distributed as (image-URL, caption) TSV; "We do not own any of the images in the dataset and hence cannot legally provide them to you"; images can disappear when owners delete them. — [CC12M GitHub](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/conceptual-12m)
- PD12M: 12.4M image-text pairs of public-domain and CC0 images with synthetic captions, "designed for training text-to-image models", with community governance; no diffusion results reported in the abstract. — [PD12M (arXiv 2410.23144)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23144)
- Re-LAION-5B was released 30 Aug 2024 as a cleaned LAION-5B (links to suspected CSAM removed after the Dec 2023 Stanford Internet Observatory report), 5,526,641,167 pairs, in "Research" and "Research-Safe" variants, under Apache-2.0. — [LAION blog](https://laion.ai/blog/relaion-5b/); [TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2024/08/30/the-org-behind-the-data-set-used-to-train-stable-diffusion-claims-it-has-removed-csam)
- DataComp pools: metadata under CC-BY-4.0, images keep their owners' copyrights; a third-party mirror labels a copy cc-by-nc-nd-4.0. — [DataComp pools card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/mlfoundations/datacomp_pools/blob/main/README.md)
- JourneyDB: 4,429,295 labelled Midjourney images (4,692,751 total), each with the generating prompt, a GPT-3.5 caption and VQA; 3.29 TB; "available under the customised Terms of Usage", gated. — [JourneyDB card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/JourneyDB/JourneyDB)
- naclbit's danbooru2021 256px guided-diffusion checkpoint: same flags as the 256 uncond ADM, "trained with 4.8M images from the danbooru2021 dataset for about 22 epochs" on 8x A100 80GB; usable in Disco Diffusion with CLIP guidance. — [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/naclbit/danbooru2021_256px_guided_diffusion)
- KaliYuga's pixelartdiffusion4k: unconditional 256x256 diffusion model trained on about 4,100 hand-picked pixel-art pieces (cc-by-3.0, attribution required for tools). — [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/KaliYuga/pixelartdiffusion4k)
- ControlNet training "does not collapse with limited 1k images" and is robust from small to large (>1M) datasets; the competitive depth model used 200k samples. — [ControlNet (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2302.05543)
- LAFITE trained language-free on MS-COCO images alone to FID 18.04, and KNN-Diffusion's FID barely changed with 10% of a 69M index (13.92 vs 13.65). — [LAFITE](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.13792); [KNN-Diffusion](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.02849)
- CLOOB-LD's overfitting rule of thumb: parameters should not exceed about 2/3 of the float count of the dataset (32×32×4 latent floats × images). — [cloob-latent-diffusion README](https://github.com/JD-P/cloob-latent-diffusion)

### Inferences
- Applying the CLOOB-LD rule to pixels (196,608 floats per 256x256x3 image): 553M parameters ≤ 2/3 × 196,608 × N → N ≳ 4,200 images for a full fine-tune, matching KaliYuga's ~4,100-image fine-tune. Conditioning needs coverage of many concepts, so 100k–1M diverse images is a more useful target; with a frozen trunk the overfitting floor is much lower. (Estimate.)
- Mix ImageNet replay (the model's own domain; conditioning on CLIP image embeddings of ImageNet images teaches the embedding→content mapping where the trunk is already good) with a broad aesthetic set (PD12M first for licence cleanliness, then CC12M/Re-LAION subsets filtered with the repo's aesthetic predictor `sac_public_2022_06_29_vit_b_32_linear.pth`). Fetching 1M URL-listed images at 256px is ~30–60 GB (estimate at 30–60 kB/image) and img2dataset-style downloading.
- Synthetic distillation is attractive here: the repo's `-bb` pipeline already produces the 0.35–0.36-scoring images. Generating N prompts × images gives perfectly aligned (text, image) pairs to train on text embeddings with no modality gap, and pulls the pixel model toward the stronger prior. Cost and licence of SD-Turbo/SDXL-Lightning outputs were not checked (see Gaps).
- Moving beyond ImageNet shifts the prior: the gain is concepts ImageNet lacks (people, text, scenes, art styles); the risk is losing ImageNet object fidelity (see Q6). Keep a fixed fraction (e.g. 20–30%) of ImageNet in every batch (estimate).

### Gaps
- ImageNet's terms (non-commercial research) and SD-Turbo / SDXL-Lightning output licences were not fetched in this pass.
- LAION-Aesthetics subsets' status under Re-LAION was not established.
- No source gives a data-size vs CLIP-score curve for conditioning a pretrained pixel model.

## 6. Risks: forgetting, diversity loss, overfitting, and how earlier work avoided them

### Takeaway
The main defences in the literature are zero-initialised new paths (ControlNet, unCLIP-style additive conditioning) so training starts from the exact pretrained model, frozen trunks, short low-lr fine-tunes with EMA, and condition dropout so an unconditional branch survives; CFG itself trades diversity for fidelity (GLIDE vs unCLIP), and CLIP-guided/CLIP-conditioned scores can be inflated when the judge shares the CLIP model.

### Cited Findings
- ControlNet's zero convolutions keep the locked model's output unchanged at the start ("harmful noise cannot influence the hidden states"), and training "does not collapse with limited 1k images". — [ControlNet (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2302.05543)
- unCLIP found GLIDE at guidance scale 2.0 matched it on photorealism and caption similarity but produced less diverse samples; guiding the prior hurt. — [unCLIP (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.06125)
- GLIDE: CLIP guidance may be "finding adversarial examples for the evaluation CLIP model". — [GLIDE (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.10741)
- LAFITE: fine-tuning a pretrained LafiteG on COCO reached FID 20.89, worse than training from scratch on COCO (18.04), while the trainable-perturbation variant fine-tuned to 17.44. — [LAFITE (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.13792)
- glide-finetune: bf16 + `torch.compile` slowly degrades outputs "into abstract blobs". — [glide-finetune README](https://github.com/afiaka87/glide-finetune)
- CLOOB-LD advises lowering lr (example 3e-5 → 3e-6) after convergence and resuming diverging fp16 runs in fp32. — [cloob-latent-diffusion README](https://github.com/JD-P/cloob-latent-diffusion)

### Inferences
- Evaluation leakage: if the model is conditioned on CLIP ViT-B/16 embeddings and scored by ViT-B/16, the score will overstate the gain (same failure GLIDE noted for CLIP guidance). Condition on one model (e.g. ViT-B/32 or a PE-Core model) and judge with the repo's held-out models, and add FID/precision-recall or human A/B on a fixed prompt set.
- Forgetting/diversity checks: track ImageNet-class FID (or CLIP similarity on ImageNet class prompts) and LPIPS/embedding diversity across seeds alongside the held-out CLIP score; stop or lower lr when ImageNet metrics fall. Using the frozen original as the CFG unconditional branch (Q3) is an extra safety net.
- Determinism: the repo requires byte-identical seeds; new layers must avoid nondeterministic kernels (e.g. use the repo's plain attention for any new cross-attention, deterministic cuDNN), per CLAUDE.md's determinism section.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies forgetting when conditioning an ImageNet ADM on web data.

## 7. Prior work that fine-tuned OpenAI's guided-diffusion ImageNet checkpoints (community and Disco Diffusion era)

### Takeaway
Community work on these checkpoints was almost entirely unconditional domain fine-tunes used with CLIP guidance at sampling (Disco Diffusion custom models, pixel art, danbooru), plus the 8,100-step unconditional fine-tune of the 512 class-conditional model; no public recipe was found that added CLIP or text conditioning to the ImageNet ADM checkpoints. Text/CLIP-conditioned pixel models of this era (cc12m_1, GLIDE) were trained from scratch with multi-GPU compute.

### Cited Findings
- `512x512_diffusion_uncond_finetune_008100.pt` is a 512x512 unconditional ImageNet model "fine-tuned for 8100 steps from the OpenAI trained 512x512 class-conditional ImageNet diffusion model", MIT licence, 2.23 GB; the card does not name Crowson as author. — [HF lowlevelware](https://huggingface.co/lowlevelware/512x512_diffusion_unconditional_ImageNet)
- danbooru2021 256px: unconditional, ADM 256 uncond flags, 4.8M images × ~22 epochs on 8x A100 80GB, card does not say whether it was initialised from OpenAI's checkpoint. — [HF naclbit](https://huggingface.co/naclbit/danbooru2021_256px_guided_diffusion)
- KaliYuga pixelartdiffusion4k: unconditional 256x256, ~4,100 images; the card gives no base model, steps or hardware. — [HF KaliYuga](https://huggingface.co/KaliYuga/pixelartdiffusion4k)
- GLIDE's ADM-derived text model is 3.5B parameters trained 2.5M iterations at batch 2048; the released filtered model is 300M (1.1M + 500K iterations). — [GLIDE (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.10741)
- cc12m_1: 602M, 3.1M steps on CC12M + 250K CFG steps, compute from stability.ai. — [v-diffusion-pytorch](https://github.com/crowsonkb/v-diffusion-pytorch)

### Inferences
- The 8,100-step 512 fine-tune shows the trunk tolerates a change of conditioning structure (class → none) in a few thousand steps; the reverse (none → CLIP embedding) through a zero-init path is plausibly similarly fast to "turn on" (ControlNet's <10K-step sudden convergence), with quality then improving slowly with data. (Inference.)
- Repurposing GLIDE-filtered (300M, text-conditioned, 64px base + upsampler, the user's own glide-finetune repo) is the alternative to conditioning ADM, but its filtered training set and 64→256 cascade make it a weaker starting point for photoreal 256px than ADM-256 (inference, not measured).

### Gaps
- Training recipes (base checkpoint, steps, lr, hardware) for Pixel Art Diffusion, Watercolor Diffusion, Portrait Generator and other Disco-era fine-tunes were not found publicly; Alex Spirin's fine-tuning notebook was not located.
- Who trained the 512 unconditional fine-tune is not confirmed by its card.
